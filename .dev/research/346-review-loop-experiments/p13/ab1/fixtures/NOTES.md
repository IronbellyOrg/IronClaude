# O1 fixture authoring decisions (PN1)

Generated by `offline/build_fixtures.py`; every anchor is verified with `findings.locate` (loaded from the PN1 clone) and the check is printed (all 29 anchors found, 1 ambiguous on purpose: `a3-r5 F-3` quotes `grep -E '^-[^-]'`, which occurs in two table rows).

- Scope: every BLOCKING finding of `round1.md` for a3-r1..a3-r6, the NEW-1 finding of `a3-r6/round2.md`, and the prior lines (status as the report states it: all `resolved`; NEW-1 `open` when it is raised, `resolved` in round 3). Medium and minor findings are not written (the plan asks for blocking ones).
- Spec version reviewed: R1 = the original overlay spec; R2 = `specs/history/spec-v2.md` for a3-r6 (the spec after V1) and the run's final spec for a3-r1..r5 (no spec-v2 exists: V1 produced the final spec); R3 (a3-r6) = the final spec.
- Anchors: the shortest exact span that the finding quotes or whose cited line it rests on; `anchor2` is the other side of a contradiction (the quoted text of the second cited line). Cited lines were read from the report's own "Text as written" and grading sentences.
- `refs` are the requirement ids the report names for that finding (WC-n, AC-n.n); `claim` is a one-sentence paraphrase. Claims were written WITHOUT inventing ids, but some mention a phase (P0/P1): under contract C5 `P<digit>` counts as a ref token.
- `severity`: the report's own label (CRITICAL/MAJOR). `category`: unpassable-check (an acceptance check cannot pass), contradiction (two stated texts), other (a check that does not detect what it claims).
- Prior lines carry the report's own ids (`F1`, `REQ-1` ...); `replay.py` translates them to ledger ids (`L1` ...) through the round-1 member list. They never change a status.
- Fix maps: all revisions recorded `Applied` for every blocking id (revise1.md/revise2.md). `replay.py` writes one `fixmap-v1` line per brief id of the ledger (a ledger row can hold several report ids: see the next point).
- Observed clustering effect (relevant for the live phase): within one report, findings whose anchors sit within 3 lines and that share a ref token are one ledger row. Because `P0`/`P1` are ref tokens, a3-r1 (F1, F2), a3-r5 (F-1, F-2) and a3-r6 (REQ-1, ARCH-1) each collapse two distinct reported defects into one row (single linkage). Their members are all kept (`every input finding appears exactly once as a member`), and the merged report quotes each member under the same lid, but the brief carries one id for two defects and one touched hunk resolves the row. The O3 cross-run merge of a3-r1, a3-r3 and a3-r6 collapses 8 findings into 2 rows for the same reason.
