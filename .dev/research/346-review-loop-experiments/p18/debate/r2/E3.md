## 1. The four other integrations

**E1 (fix-closure).** Strongest claim: it is the only integration acting after the reviser, where the ledger shows the loss, and its smoke run closed 8 of 8 correct fixes. Attack: the smoke stop fired (3 false closures: sm-19, sm-20, sm-24; `p15/e1/results.json`). The 35.6% saving (11,049 to 7,113 tokens) is measured against a per-finding conventional checker that ab5 does not have. ab4 and ab5 re-check fixes inside R2/R3. E1 therefore adds a step, about +30k by E1's own estimate, and the net is "roughly neutral". Jev can only close. A refusal at R3 leaves a finding open with no third fix, so E1 cannot raise `fixed of 44`. E1's patch, "mapped hunk overlaps two open blockers: NOT_ELIGIBLE", was invented after seeing the failures. By the E2 precedent ("no levers after outcomes", `p15/e2/results.json` notes) it needs a held-out rerun.

**E2 (reading priorities).** Strongest claim: it is the only one with a passing headroom stage (H=3/16). Attack: `p15/e2/results.json` has status `blocked`, not passed. Pool misses were 11 against a limit of 2, so Stage 1 and live are blocked, exactly like E1. E2's "least gate-negative" framing is wrong. Only 5/16 evidence pairs reach the pool, and static coverage was 2/16. Stage 0 made zero Jev calls, so Jev's contribution is untested. The four-reviewer panel already raises D06-D12; `perdefect.md` shows them fixed in 0-6 of 6 specs, with the failure at revise. E2 aims at raising, not fixing.

**E4.** Strongest claim: it is end-to-end use of E1 with a conventional fallback. Attack: it is E1 plus its gate failure. E4 also needs a conventional narrow-check step that ab5 lacks, so its 7-14k saving (1.7-3.5%) is against a baseline cost that does not exist.

**E5.** Strongest claim: it is nearly free as a shadow observer. Attack: headroom is 1/11 (`p15/e5/results.json`) and `needs_assurance=yes` is 10/11. Nothing is measurable in a shadow arm. E5 concedes it should not be in the top 3, and I agree.

## 2. Defence of E3

**Conceded.**
- E3 is NO-GO-OFFLINE (automation 0.179 against a 0.20 minimum). Jev's false-confirmation rate was 0.30 against 0.20 for the conventional verifier. The `outcome_follows >= 0.7` bin was only 54% blocking (`p15/e3/offline-results.md`).
- The saving is tiny. VF1 output is 37,901 tokens over 6 ab4 runs (6.3k mean, 43 merged findings, about 0.9k each). At 18% automation that is about 1.1k per run, under 0.3% of ab5's roughly 408k. VF reads the spec once, so cost is sub-linear in findings and the real saving is lower.
- E3 cannot move residual blocking, so it is invisible at n=6.
- Panel agreement (2 or more tiers) is a free, stronger signal; E3 duplicates it.

**Correction to my own round-1 paper.** I said VF might wrongly reject the multi-source defects D06, D07, D11 and D12. That was wrong. The ab4 VF logs (`p16/ab4/runs/d4-r*/state.json`) show 8 rejections in 43 findings. By their text, four match ledger blockers: D01 (r1 M4, r4 M1, both the AC-1.1/P1-scope vs D3(a) shape) and D27 (r4 M8, r5 M6, "no path reaches succeeded"). In r1 and r4, D01 and D02 stayed PRESENT in the final spec (`p17/score/out/s45.json`, `s65.json`). So VF over-rejection does cost blockers.

**That does not rescue E3.** Every one of those rejections says "needs an assumption the text does not state". These are inferential findings, which are exactly where `texts_conflict` and `outcome_follows` are uncertain. E3 would route them to VF unchanged. The fix for VF over-rejection is a code rule, or a VF brief change that exempts findings raised by 2 or more tiers. It is not Jev. This is a free ab5 tweak outside the Jev slots.

**Foreseeable attack: "harm is bounded."** Only partly true. A falsely confirmed finding reaches the reviser, which edits the spec for a non-defect. This feeds the 0.3-1.3 introduced-defect hits per spec and the WORSE/MOOT_BAD counts. Harm is bounded, not zero.

## 3. Final recommendation

I would drop E3 as a ledger-moving integration. Ordering for ab5:

1. **E1, with E4 folded in as the same build.** It is the only candidate at the revise-to-close seam where ab5 loses. It is conditional: the owner must accept a pre-registered NOT_ELIGIBLE patch with a held-out rerun.
2. **E2.** It is cheap and cannot cause false closure; the fallback is document-order hints. It needs an owner amendment to the pool-miss rule. Expect no detectable ledger effect.
3. **E3.** Only as a no-regression filler, because E4 shares E1's slot and E5 is shadow-only. It is safe but near-valueless, and it needs a rerun on ab5's merged panel findings before it could ever go live. If the owner has a better use for the slot, such as the code-side VF exemption above, take that instead.
