# A3 (revision novelty): advocate paper, round 2

Verdict: A3 belongs in the top 3 only narrowly. It is the one seam with no false-closure path, near-zero cost and a runnable headroom test. It is not a quality lever the ledger could detect.

## 1. Mechanism and insertion point
The "proposed V2 revision" does not exist when the V2 brief is written, so the question is asked one step earlier. After R2 FAILs and before V2, code builds a packet per surviving blocker: the V1 hunks mapped to its span, R2's re-raise text, and the ledger history (`named_not_touched`). Jev answers {new aspect, same defect restated, no relevant change, insufficient}. The answer goes into the V2 brief as one flag-only line. An optional post-V2 label is recorded for measurement only. Fallback: `insufficient`, error or timeout gives no hint (today's path). The cap stays at 2 fixes.

## 2. Headroom (files opened)
- **Applicability.** In ab4 the loop never reached V2: all six `round2.md` end `verdict: PASS` (d4-r1..r6) and `state.json` done-lists stop at R2. In PN1, 5 of 6 runs reached V2 (b1-r3 passed at R2). ab5 is panel-first then ab4-style, so V2 fires between 0/6 and 5/6, unmeasured.
- **Survivors.** PN1 ledgers (`p13/ab1/runs/b1-r*/ws/.dev/*/review/ledger.json`): 23 blockers in V1 briefs, 10 re-briefed at V2, all 10 marked "applied" at V1. V2 resolved 2 of 10 (b1-r4 L2, b1-r6 L2). V1 resolved 13 of 23. Five survivors carry `named_not_touched`; 0 of those 5 resolved. The other 5 resolved 2.
- **Ledger defects.** Final-spec status counts (`score/out/*.json` with `key.json`): PN1 blocking residual cells 60 of 120, of which PARTIAL (touched but incomplete) 32 (53%). ab4: 86 residual, PARTIAL 19 (22%), PRESENT 67. Blocking defects PARTIAL in 3+ of 6 PN1 specs: D03, D08, D09, D10, D26, D31 (6 of 20 blocking, 6 of 44). In ab4 the same test gives D04, D08, D09. D08-D10 are the common core: the contract-path umbrella finding L1, which survived V1 in all 5 PN1 runs that reached V2.
- **Control.** `named_not_touched` already labels half the survivors. Touched-line overlap: V2 replaced or deleted 42 lines, 27 (64%) of them lines V1 had touched (5/8, 6/7, 5/11, 5/9, 6/7). It discriminates nothing. PN2 lint (1.5 fixed, no effect) does not inspect revisions.
- **Upside.** Lifting survivor conversion from 20% to 40% is +2 lids over 6 runs, 0.33 per run. L1 bundles up to five defects (D06-D10), so +0.3 to +1.7 defects per spec against a detectable 3.

## 4. Complementarity and the competitor
It touches nothing E2, E3, E5 or the panel touch. It shares its packet with E1/E4 (diffs and spans). A1 acts at V1 on 23 blockers, 2.3 times A3's 10.

Strongest competitor: **E1**, the only other lever on the revise seam. `p15/e1/results.json` and `e1/offline-results.md`: SMOKE NO-GO-OFFLINE, 3 false AUTO_CLOSEs (sm-19, sm-20, sm-24) against a limit of 0, with 12 AUTO_CLOSEs in total and 8 true. ## 5. Strongest argument against A3 and my answer
**Against.** The ledger does not show restated fixes. It shows a mutating umbrella finding. In b1-r5, V1 touched L1's sub-points (adversarial path, AC-1.1, retry overwrite, status values). R2 re-raised L1 as new sub-findings N1-N9 (`revise1.md`, `revise2.md`), and V2 applied N1-N5 and N9 and rejected N8 as "unstated requirements", the same rejection V1 made. Jev would label V2 "new aspect" and the hint changes nothing. Add n=10, half covered by `named_not_touched`, an unmeasured V2 firing rate, and ESCALATE after R3 in 5 of 5 PN1 runs.

**Answer.** I concede the first-order effect is about 0. The defence is only that survivors are concentrated (L1 in 5 runs; D08-D10 PARTIAL in both ab4 and PN1), so one hint could name the structural cure (fix the output-location rule, not each path) rather than another reword. That is speculative. A3 ranks third because the field is gate-failed (E1, E3, E5) or conceded as the wrong lever (E2, `r2/E2.md`). If the owner demands a detectable effect, A3 drops too.
