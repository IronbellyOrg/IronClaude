## 1. The other four

**E1.** Strongest claim: it is the only integration at the revise-to-re-review seam, and it had 0 false closures on all 20 deleted-obligation, moved-defect and partial-branch cases. Attacks:
- Every smoke case is synthetic. `p15/e1/cases_smoke.py` tags all cases `data_classes: ["synthetic"]`, with invented families (refunds, invoice_numbering). No ledger defect (D01-D12) was ever tested. The paper's "persistent defects fit E1's target" is inference, not measurement.
- The gate stopped on 3 false AUTO_CLOSEs of 12 (`p15/e1/results.json`: sm-19, sm-20, sm-24). Zero was the pre-registered limit. The proposed code rule ("mapped hunk overlaps two open blockers: NOT_ELIGIBLE") is a lever added after seeing the outcome. E1 admits the E2 precedent forbids this.
- A false AUTO_CLOSE removes the finding from R2/R3, and no fourth review exists to catch it. The "+0% to +7% tokens" prediction is not a saving.

**E2.** Strongest claim: Stage 0 shows headroom (H=3 of 16) and the least bad gate record. Attacks:
- `p15/e2/results.json` has `status: blocked` and verdict BLOCKED, not "passed". Candidate coverage is 5/16 and 11 pool misses exceed the limit of 2.
- Two of the three headroom cases are seeded synthetic chains (s0-21, s0-23 are `seeded` in `offline/stage0-results.md`). Only s0-22 is real, and it is D16, a medium defect fixed in 1 of 6 specs, in PN1 only.
- Zero Jev calls were made, so the Jev contribution is unmeasured.
- It improves raising, but the panel already raises, and the ledger says the loss is after raising.

**E3.** Strongest claim: structurally zero blocker loss, because Jev can only confirm. Attacks:
- The gate failed on automation (0.179 vs 0.20). Worse, Jev's false-confirmation rate is 0.30 against the conventional verifier's 0.20 (`p15/e3/results.json`, 3 of 10 vs 2 of 10). The `outcome_follows` 0.7+ bin is only 54% blocking.
- Its own ceiling is about 0.4% of tokens. It also duplicates what ab5's panel agreement and ADJ already provide.
- Zero blockers lost is not zero harm. A false confirmation sends an invalid finding to the reviser, which feeds the introduced-defect hits (0.3-1.3 per spec).

**E5.** Strongest claim: honest, nearly free as a shadow observer. Attack: `p15/e5/results.json` has headroom 1/11 packets, and that packet (a2-r6) has a single PASS audit. The audits sample about 7% of the blocking defects (`p17/LEDGER-RESULTS.md`), so even that one is not evidence. Live use needs a scope amendment, and shadow mode cannot show any effect at n=6. I agree with its self-drop.

## 2. Defence of E4, and concessions

Attacks on E4, with my answers:
- **"E4 is E1 under another name, so it takes a slot for nothing."** True, and I concede it. The component, packets and thresholds are the same. `PLAN-E1E4` says E4 adds only the live B/C arms. E4 should not be a separate integration.
- **"The gate is NO-GO."** Fully conceded. E4 is blocked on E1's NO-GO-OFFLINE, and the cases were synthetic and single-reviewer. I also dispute my own round-1 claim that D01, D02 and D06-D12 "survive in almost every arm". `p17/score/perdefect.md` shows D01 fixed 6/6 in ab3, D06 4/6 in the baseline, and D07 6/6 in ab4 and PN1. D09/D10 are 0 in nearly every arm because they were never raised. They are not cases of "found, then survived".
- **"E4 cannot raise `fixed`."** True. Jev can only close, and the reviser is untouched. The only quality route is arm C, a strict conventional closure check, and that is not a Jev contribution.
- **"The saving is 1.7-3.5%."** `PLAN-E1E4` line 285 itself expects the ADOPT bar (B <= 0.80 x C loop tokens) to fail, with only 5-8% of C's loop at stake. I find nothing in the files to rescue the E4 cost claim.
- One point holds. In the E1 smoke, the conventional checker also falsely closed sm-20 and sm-24. A strict, packet-structured conventional check is therefore not safe either. So the "Jev closes" step is not the whole risk, and any closure design needs an independent audit of what it closes.

Verdict: drop E4 as a separate pick.

1. **E1 (with E4's live harness as the same build), only if re-pre-registered with the hunk-overlap rule and a held-out rerun on ledger-derived packets (D06-D12 edits from real ab5 revisions), not synthetic.** It is the only candidate at the seam where the ledger shows the loss. Expect an inconclusive score; judge it mainly on zero false closures and the auto-close share.
2. **E2.** It is additive, adds no steps downstream, and its gate result is a blocked headroom pass, not a failure. Run it as a cheap hint, expecting only a small gain on cross-section defects that no arm raises (D14-D17).
3. **E3, as a cost-trim control only.** It is the lowest-risk slot. Its 0.179 near miss was measured on single-reviewer findings and may clear 0.20 on panel-merged findings. Expect about 0.4% token saving and no score effect. It is a weak third, but E5 is dead and E4 duplicates E1.

None of these three is likely to move residual blocking by the roughly 3 that n=6 can detect. The honest summary is that the panel and a stronger revise step are the levers, and Jev integrations are cost or safety add-ons at best.
