## 2. Defence of E1

**The false closures.** Fully conceded, and worse than my round-1 paper said. I re-opened `p15/e1/offline/replay-smoke.jsonl`. All four C7 (coupled-blocker) cases auto-closed (sm-19, sm-20, sm-24, sm-38), 4 of 4, each with `n_deps` = 0 and defect-present 0.03-0.17. The 12 AUTO_CLOSEs are 4 C1, 4 C4 and 4 C7. The gate stop was correct and the failure class is complete, not marginal. The conventional checker also falsely closed sm-20 and sm-24 (`results.json`), so ab5's current re-review has the same blind spot. That is an argument that the weakness is real, not that Jev is excused.

**My "exact code detection" claim is only partly verified.** In `cases_smoke.py` (C7, the invoice family) the damaged obligation is IN-4, a passage that was never retrieved as a dependency. The root flaw is dependency-retrieval recall. A rule "hunk overlaps two open blockers" catches C7 only if the broken passage is itself an open finding. I have not shown that. So a patch needs a held-out rerun, not just a rule.

**Headroom in ab5 is smaller than I implied.** In ab4 all 6 runs ended R1, VF1, V1, R2 PASS, then audit (`p16/ab4/runs/d4-r*/state.json`). The second fix cycle was never used, and 14.3 blocking defects remained. That is the strongest case for a stricter closure check. But PN1 already reaches V2 in 5 of 6 runs (`p13/ab1/runs/b1-r*/state.json`). With the panel, R3 PASS then arrives with 10 blockers left. At R3 a stricter check has no cycle to trigger, so E1's lever is largely spent at R2. Its gain in ab5 is smaller than in ab4.

**Jev contributes only tokens.** Jev can only close. Anything that keeps defects open comes from the packet and conventional fallback. A fair test of E1 is therefore "packet checker with Jev auto-close" against "packet checker without it". The predicted effect of about -1 blocking is below the ~3 detectable at n=6. I said so, and I keep it.

**Cost.** About +5% tokens (net of the 35.6% narrow-stage saving) for no measurable score gain. That is poor on criterion 3.
