# E5 opening case (evidence-sufficiency routing)

Position up front: this is the weakest of the five integrations for ab5. I argue it as strongly as the evidence lets me, and I concede where it fails.

## 1. Mechanism
E5 acts at a provisional PASS: a re-review (or first review) returns PASS before the 3-review / 2-fix cap. The router chooses between delivering the spec and spending the one remaining review slot on a whole-document assurance review. Code first removes every decision that hashes, caps or policy already fix (cap reached, open blocker, unchanged spec hash). Jev would then answer a typed Choice (`answered` / `conflicting` / `not_answered`) plus a Noul `evidence_relevant`. The only permitted down-route is deliver-without-assurance, and only when `evidence_relevant >= 0.7` and `answered >= 0.7` with no guard active. Anything else takes the static route. In ab5 this would sit after the scoped re-review R2/R3 with lint. It never touches the panel, merge, ADJ, clean VF or revise. It adds no finding and no fix, so it cannot change the revise bottleneck. Its only possible effect is to skip a review. The plan (p15/plans/PLAN-E5-assurance-headroom.md) is shadow-only. Live use needs an owner cap-neutral scope amendment.

## 2. Evidence it addresses a real failure
It does not address the measured failure. p17/LEDGER-RESULTS.md says reviewers raise defects but revise leaves them unresolved (baseline fixes 2.8 of 44; PN1 leaves 10.0 of 20 blocking). No ledger id (D01-D44) is touched by a decision about whether to run a further review. E5's one measured result is negative: p15/e5/results.json has verdict NO-GO-OFFLINE with headroom 1/11 eligible packets (9.1%, below the 2-packet and 10% minima). `needs_assurance` is "yes" for 10 of 11 eligible packets (91%). The single headroom packet is a2-r6, which had one PASS audit (p15/e5/offline/headroom.md). The ledger weakens even that one: a frozen audit samples roughly 7% of the blocking defects present, so a lone PASS audit is not evidence of adequacy. True headroom is likely 0.

Does the gate still apply to ab5? Yes, and more strongly. A provisional PASS from a scoped re-review is the same event, and the ledger shows 14-17 blocking defects left in nearly every arm. Where residual blockers are the norm, "always use the slot" is the right rule and a router can only lose.

## 3. Complementarity
E5 reinforces nothing in ab5. It is orthogonal to the panel, lint, the AC sweep and the clean VF, and none of them depends on it. Among the integrations, E1/E4 (fix closure) and E3 (verifier skip) act earlier and on the actual bottleneck. E2 acts on first-review reading. E5 duplicates only the idea of "spending less where safe", already covered by E3. If it ran together with E1, the two would compete for the same re-review slot.

## 4. Cost
Shadow probe: 96 Jev calls, under $0.01 (plan section 3). Tokens saved on ab5: the best case saves one assurance re-review of about 30k tokens (plan section 3) on headroom packets only. Expected saving per run is about 0.09 (headroom rate) x 30k = 2.7k tokens, against ~408k panel loops (0.7%) or ~106k ab4-style loops (2.5%). That is far below the plan's own 15% bar. Nothing is added to ab5 if it stays shadow-only. If it is made live, it adds a failure mode: skipping a review on a spec that is probably still defective.

## 5. Strongest case against, and why it sinks it
The case against: the premise is false. The gate shows no router can beat static routing, the ledger shows residual blocking in nearly every arm, and the live stage is blocked by scope. I do not think this is answerable. The honest counter is that E5 is nearly free and could be run purely as a shadow observer to learn whether a PASS-time signal exists in ab5. But the ab6 slot is a scarce one. n=6 and an effect of about 3 blocking defects are detectable, and a shadow observer yields zero detectable effect. So I concede E5 should not be in the top 3.

## 6. Falsifiable prediction
If I am right that E5 is not worth a slot, ab6 with E5 (shadow) matches ab5 on both metrics:
- Residual blocking stays within sd of about 2 of ab5, and fixed of 44 within noise (no gain above 3).
- Loop tokens stay within ±1% of ab5, since no assurance review is ever skipped.
- At least 90% of observed PASS states are labeled needs-assurance by the ledger.
If instead a live E5 showed 15% or more loop-token savings with no rise in residual blocking, that would refute this concession. I assign it under 5% probability.
