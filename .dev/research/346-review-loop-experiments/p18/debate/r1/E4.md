# E4 opening case (advocate)

Verdict first: E4 is the only integration aimed at the revise-to-re-review seam, which is where the ledger says ab5 loses. It is also gated behind a failed offline result. My case is conditional, and I say so below.

## 1. Mechanism
E4 plugs into ab5 at the scoped re-reviews R2/R3 only. For each prior blocking finding, harness code builds a packet: the cited obligation, a before/after diff, verbatim flags, and up to 3 retrieved dependency passages. Jev answers `defect_still_present`, `obligation_still_required`, `contradicts_dK` and `context_sufficient`. Only an all-clear (present<0.3, obligation>=0.7, contradiction<0.3, context>=0.7) AUTO_CLOSEs the finding, and the conventional re-reviewer skips it. Everything else (noop, unmapped, oversize, a Jev error, any uncertainty) goes to the conventional checker on the same packet. Jev never keeps a finding open, never writes PASS, and adds no review slot. Because the packet separates "defect removed" from "obligation preserved", the conventional fallback in arm C is also a stricter closure check than today's re-review (PLAN-E1E4 section 1).

## 2. Evidence
- Failure mode: `p17/LEDGER-RESULTS.md` says reviewers raise the defects but the revise step does not resolve them. D01, D02, D04, D06-D12 survive in almost every arm (`p17/score/perdefect.md`; D08-D10 are 0-3 of 6 in every arm except pn1). A closure check that refuses partial or deleted-obligation fixes targets that seam.
- PLAN-E1E4 estimated about 4 of 9 first-audit blockers as "found, then survived".
- Offline gate, `p15/e1/offline-results.md`: 34 eligible cases, 12 AUTO_CLOSE (coverage 0.35), narrow-stage output tokens down 35.6%, Jev status 34/34 ok, p95 211 ms, closing decision stable under perturbation 10/10 groups, USD 0.0019.
- The gate is **NO-GO-OFFLINE**: three false AUTO_CLOSEs (sm-19, sm-20, sm-24) against the pre-registered limit of zero. The conventional checker also closed sm-20 and sm-24 falsely (agreement with Jev 0.97), so two of the three errors are not Jev-specific. They show a hard class of case (distant dependency, partial fix), not only a Jev flaw. That does not rescue the gate.

## 3. Complementarity
- ab5's clean VF screens first-review findings. E4 acts after the revise step on fix closure. They do not overlap.
- The panel (4 tiers) raises recall, and PN1's win is mostly recall followed by more revise fixes. E4 sits downstream and does not duplicate it.
- Lint and the AC sweep sit upstream, so they are orthogonal.
- E4 reinforces E1: E4 is the end-to-end live use of E1's component, so they are one build. It duplicates E3 in spirit (Jev confirms, conventional path decides), but at a different stage. It does not combine with E2, which acts on first reviewers.
- Since E4 depends on E1, picking E4 means picking E1's component, so it takes one slot, not two.

## 4. Cost
The saving is small and bounded by the narrow checks. Conventional checker cost was 11,049 output tokens over 34 packets (about 325 each). The plan puts a checker at 10-20k per revision. With 2 revisions and a measured 36% narrow-stage reduction, the saving is 2 x (10-20k) x 0.36 = 7-14k tokens, against ab5 loop tokens of about 408k (the panel alone is about 340k). That is 1.7-3.5%. Jev dollars are 34 calls = $0.0019, so about $0.01 per run. If the gate failed in live use, the extra cost is the C-style checker, perhaps +5-10k. Net is token-neutral, with no quality gain unless C's stricter closure helps.

## 5. Strongest case against
(a) The gate failed. By the owner's rules E4 is blocked until E1 passes smoke, calibration and held-out, so it cannot be in ab6 as designed. (b) Jev can only close, never fix. The ledger problem is that the reviser does not fix, and E4 changes nothing about revising. It can save tokens or prevent false closure, but it cannot raise "fixed of 44". (c) The saving is about 2-3% and below detectability. (d) The gate was measured on 40 synthetic cases against the old single-reviewer loop. Panel plus lint changes the finding mix, so the result may or may not transfer. The false-closure failures (distant dependency, partial fix) are properties of packet-only judgment, which ab5 does not change, so I treat the gate as still applying.

I do not think these sink it as a conditional candidate. (b) and (c) mean E4 cannot be the headline. It belongs in the top 3 only if the owner wants a cost-side, safe-fallback integration and accepts re-running E1 with a revised threshold policy (the calibration grid of 256 policies was never run, because smoke stopped first). Honestly, on the brief's criteria E4 scores low on 1 and 3 and fails 4. If E2 (Stage 0 passed) and one other integration are stronger, E4 should be third at best.

## 6. Falsifiable prediction
If E4 is right and E1 is revised to zero false closures: residual blocking is within +/-2 of ab5 (no detectable change at n=6, sd about 2), fixed of 44 is unchanged within noise, loop tokens drop by 7-14k (1.7-3.5%, ab5 about 408k to about 395-401k), and zero attributable false closures. The claim "closure check improves fixed" is refuted if arm C (strict conventional checker) shows no residual-blocking reduction of 3 or more vs ab5. If B shows any attributable false closure, E4 is dropped (DROP-1).
