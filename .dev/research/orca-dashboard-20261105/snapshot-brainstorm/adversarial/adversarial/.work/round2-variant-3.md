# Round 2 Rebuttal: Variant 3 Advocate (haiku:backend)

## Response to Criticisms

V1's strongest case remains structural: its owns/must-never boundaries and shared frame make the truth contract reusable across renderers (V1 sections 2 and 4). V2's strongest case remains reviewability: a shared fixture, responsibility bands, drawn overflow, alternate-state risk matrix and separate reader/mechanical checks make omissions easier to challenge (V2 sections 2.6, 4.2, 7.1, 8 and 9). Both should survive the merge. The responses below address the V3 criticisms in both rival Round 1 statements and both rival Round 2 rebuttals; citations refer to the reopened original variants, not to inferred runtime behavior.

**CR-1. The concepts are too similar, weakening the evaluation alternatives (V1 Round 1 critiques 1/8; V2 Round 1 weaknesses 2; both Round 2 assessments). Conceded.** Reusing a fixture is desirable, but repeating nearly identical bundles does not demonstrate five sufficiently distinct whole-report organizations. V3's brief, handoffs and journal give the same small set too similar a visual treatment (V3 sections 5, 8 and 9). The Run Ledger adds reconciliation and the Blocker Paths adds dependencies, so the concepts are not literally identical; nevertheless V1's queue/matrix/tree/ladder/journal set is the stronger demonstration (V1 sections 5-10). On the second organizing axis specifically, V2's responsibility bands are more different from a decision queue than V3's per-run register (V2 section 4.2; V3 section 6). C-001 moves to V2; retain V3's assignment/verification/execution distinction as reconciliation content rather than a reason to prefer that axis.

**CR-2. Worker verification precedes a human question; severity allegedly makes oldest findability depend on an unverified field (V1 Round 1 critiques 2/9; V2 Round 1 weakness 1; both Round 2 ordering discussions). Concede the ordering defect, reject the dependency claim.** V3 puts worker-owned DEMO-F1 above human-owned DEMO-D2 in its default despite supplying no source severity that requires this (V3 sections 5 and 10). My Round 1 preference for V1 DEC-1 stands: all observed pending human questions should precede the worker verification block in the default (V1 section 3.1). Reserving space for a verification contradiction need not mean interleaving it above a human question. However, V3 section 4 rule 2 explicitly says that any source-backed urgency override must retain an `oldest observed` pointer. Oldest findability is therefore not conditional on severity data; without that data the override is unavailable. The severity policy itself remains unspecified, so it should not determine the initial default. V3's resource-question placement also exposes an overly narrow distinction between blocking and other human questions; the merged ordering rule must cover both.

**CR-3. No reusable frame or printed source legend (V1 Round 1 critiques 3/10; V2 Round 2 NE-6). Conceded without qualification.** V3 defines W/M/V in prose outside the snapshots, while its screens vary their header wording (V3 section 4 closing paragraph; sections 5-10). That does not meet standalone readability as well as V1's mandatory header/footer, fixed OLDEST field and printed legend (V1 sections 4.1-4.3). Retain V1's frame structure, populated with V3's source-specific freshness and census semantics. Neither shared rules nor repeated-looking headers substitute for a frame contract.

**CR-4. Flat R01-R16 lack an owning layer (V1 Round 1 critiques 4/11; V1 Round 2 assessment). Concede the missing ownership, retain the acceptance-condition contribution.** V3 section 2 answers how a reviewer could observe compliance; it does not identify which component enforces each rule. V1 section 3 answers where enforcement belongs, and V2 section 3 answers why a requirement exists through its Basis column. These are complementary, not mutually exclusive. Keep V1's architecture and add Layer and Basis traceability to observable acceptance conditions. S-003 stays V3 because an acceptance condition remains the most useful catalogue property from my reliability lens; that vote does not claim that V3 already has ownership traceability.

**CR-5. No drawn overload fixture for E06 (V1 Round 1 critiques 5/12; V2 Round 1 weakness 3; V2 Round 2 NE-6). Conceded.** V3's three-item fixture and future seven-under-six acceptance case specify accounting, but do not show the screen E06 needs (V3 sections 1 and 11). V2's dedicated 19-decision example prints shown 3 plus omitted 16 equals observed 19 (V2 section 7.1). Use that demonstrated overload structure and an explicit fixture table; extend the future fixture to include the pinned old unchanged question, overlap and a long question. Those are merge requirements, not fixtures I claim already exist in V3.

**CR-6. Five operators and an underspecified switch condition cannot establish a winning default (V1 Round 1 critiques 6/13; V1 Round 2 assessment). Partly conceded.** V3 explicitly calls its five-operator study an initial design evaluation, not statistical proof of superiority (V3 section 11). Such a pilot can reveal comprehension failures and reject unsafe wording; it cannot support a broad statistical winner claim. Thus the sample is not invalid for its stated qualitative purpose, but V3's rule lacks a declared improvement margin and tie policy, and the missing overflow fixture prevents applying E06 as written. V2 gives the stronger evaluation organization, sample-size reporting guidance, critical failures and default decision rule (V2 sections 9.1-9.3). S-009 moves to V2; keep V3's deterministic integrity cases alongside it. V2's at-least-eight guidance is also not, by itself, proof of statistical power. Its section 6 switch-to-C5 condition and section 9.3 general selection rule should be reconciled into one predeclared rule before results are interpreted.

**CR-7. No comparison matrix (V2 Round 1 weakness 3 and strengths 3; both Round 2 assessments). Conceded.** V3 section 10 has a short use-case paragraph, not a systematic comparison artifact. V1's ratings plus source-dependency table explain both readability and prerequisite costs, and should prevail (V1 sections 11.1-11.2). V2's gate-load and state-risk comparisons add useful checking dimensions (V2 sections 5 and 8). None of these ratings is operator evidence; I do not independently claim measured line counts or platform behavior from reading their tables.

**CR-8. Six bundles and a yielding 44-line target are not a hard transport bound (V2 Round 1 weakness 3; both Round 2 size discussions). Conceded.** V3's whole-bundle rule protects meaning, not message size (V3 section 4 rules 7-9). One arbitrarily long question can exceed a message limit even with only one bundle. V2's hard line budget and overflow arithmetic are stronger at bounding layout (V2 R-FMT-03, R-OVF-01 and section 7.1), but do not verify the target platform's total-message/fence limit. C-016 stays V3 for omission semantics and the separation of observed/shown/render-omitted/unobserved, not because its size bound is adequate. The merge needs both a verified transport limit and whole-bundle omission. If even the mandatory question and warnings cannot fit, the single-paste/full-question promise is infeasible for that input and must be reported as such; silently clipping required context or inventing an archive is not a repair (V3 section 4 rule 9; G06).

**CR-9. Age-first can put a one-task blocker before a four-task blocker (V2 Round 1 weakness 8; V1 Round 2 CR-5). Accepted as a cost, not a reason to rank incomplete impact estimates.** V3 section 7 deliberately forgoes unlock-size ranking without a complete dependency graph. Its example explicitly marks graph completeness unknown. Sorting incomplete impact lower bounds cannot establish which answer actually frees the most work. Age-first preserves the primary waiting-time question while showing qualified impact alongside it (V3 section 4 rules 1/6; section 7). Once a graph and authority/coverage guarantees exist, impact-first can be evaluated as an explicit alternate ordering; V1's OLDEST frame can preserve discoverability in that alternate (V1 sections 4.2, 8.1 and 17 G4). It is not justified by the current partial fixture, and complete graph data alone would still not prove an operator preference.

**CR-10. `Missing in-scope hosts: none` may overstate an inventory that G04 leaves open (V2 Round 2 NE-5). Qualified concession.** V3 section 4 explicitly defines the synthetic host inventory as only host-DEMO-1, with other hosts outside the named scope, and the empty screen names that host and disclaims work outside scope (V3 section 10). G04 is an open real-source gate, not proof that a deliberately one-host synthetic fixture is internally impossible (V3 section 12). Nevertheless the screen's terse `none` can hide the distinction between intended coverage and the scope actually queried. A real renderer must not silently redefine intended scope as the hosts it reached. Retain `none` only with evidence that the explicit expected in-scope set was fully checked; otherwise missing in-scope hosts are unknown and wider coverage remains unknown. V3's scope-qualified empty wording is still preferable to a fleet-wide all-clear, but the host line needs that stronger condition.

**CR-11. Remaining V3 omissions acknowledged in my Round 1 still stand.** The Run Ledger puts inventory before action bundles despite its own instruction to keep actions first (V3 section 6); repair the displayed order. A reply-not-resolution rule is not a visible answered-unresolved treatment; borrow V2's dedicated band (V2 sections 4.1 and 6; V3 section 4 rule 3). V3 does not define heartbeat classification, an actual per-person ownership source, unresolved-history capacity, a severity policy or meaning-preserving secret detection/redaction; its stale cutoff also needs source-specific agreement (V3 R06/R16; sections 3, 4 and 12 G05/G07). These are unresolved requirements, not solved backend capabilities. Retention exhaustion must not become a silent resolution, and redaction must not expose a secret merely to preserve verbatim wording. No product implementation is proposed in this rebuttal.

## Updated Assessment

The merge should keep three complementary contributions rather than declare a complete winning variant:

- **V1 supplies boundaries and presentation contracts:** collector/store/derivation/renderer separation, requirement ownership, fixed OLDEST and legend, distinct concept structures and dependency comparison (V1 sections 2-5 and 11.2). Its invariant is a proposed constraint, not mechanical proof that the hand-drawn screens conform.
- **V3 supplies observation integrity:** separate collection/evidence clocks; cursor failure and moving-census semantics; source/scope-compatible baselines; positive-only partial additions; unknown current state after disappearance; authority and revision provenance; task-set unions and cycle handling (V3 sections 3-4 and 7). These rules belong inside V1's boundaries, with the resulting qualifiers carried into the shared frame. Adding census/age/freshness fields is not necessarily invisible to a renderer.
- **V2 supplies review and evaluation:** evidence tags, explicit fixture, responsibility grouping, overflow screen, ANSWERED NOT RESOLVED and WATCH treatments, concept/state risk matrix, ET/MC split and binding non-consuming gate (V2 sections 1, 2.6, 4.1-4.2, 7.1, 8, 9 and 12.1). Its conformance and under-cap wording need the corrections its advocate accepts.

Three votes change from my Round 1: S-009 V3 to V2, C-001 V3 to V2, and A-002 V2 to V3. The first two distinguish evaluation structure and concept axis from V3's useful underlying data rules. The third distinguishes correct acting-owner semantics from a helpful responsibility layout. All changes are marked in the table.

I retain V1 on X-006/X-007 despite the rival advocates voting V3: the brief asks which handling should prevail, not whose document has fewer admitted inconsistencies. Retain the decisions-first invariant and mandatory oldest pointer, and repair the alternative screens; adopting V3's mixed default order merely because it is deliberate is not the desired remedy (V1 DEC-1/DEC-3; V2 R-DEC-01; V3 section 4 rule 2).

## New Evidence

**NE-1. Candidate identity is not a classifier or a visibility policy.** Both rival Round 2 A-001 discussions credit V3's candidate-question rule as keeping ambiguous messages visible. Rechecking the source supports a narrower claim: a source/run/thread/message reference may identify an observed candidate question, and missing references require uncertain identity (V3 section 3, Logical records and authority). That rule does not decide whether an ambiguous message becomes a candidate in the first place. My Round 1 explicitly noted this gap. Ambiguity-preserving classification/visibility must be an added merge requirement; the V2 WATCH row addresses silence, not classification (V2 sections 2.4 and 4.1).

**NE-2. The overlap rule is specified, not demonstrated or tested.** V2 Round 2 CR-6 describes V3's overlap promise as kept and tested. V3 section 4 rule 6 requires a note under both questions, section 7 requires multi-parent repeats and cycle conflicts, and section 11 lists future acceptance cases. However its drawn fixture has D1 holding two tasks and F1 a different third task, not two decisions sharing one task (V3 sections 1 and 7). The rule should prevail on X-009, but no executed test or drawn overlap fixture supports a stronger claim. V3 section 11 explicitly says no tests or operator trials were performed.

**NE-3. A testable VANISHED label still needs observation semantics.** V1 Round 2 NE-3 correctly finds that non-reappearance under a capped read may mean merely beyond the cap. V1's explicit disappearance category and EV-8 are useful proposals (V1 CHG-3; sections 6.2 and 14), but testability does not make message absence a genuine disappearance event. Keep the item as last-known unresolved, current status unknown, and report source non-reappearance only with its cap/coverage qualifier (V3 section 4 rules 3-5). C-010 stays V3; a verified source deletion event could be separately labeled without inferring resolution.

**NE-4. The source corrections narrow, rather than overturn, Round 1 criticisms.** V1's under-cap defect is in OVF-7's rows-equal-cap trigger and its under-cap OK examples, not a direct COV-3 requirement to call short reads complete (V1 sections 13 and 12.1). Its journal starts with NEW/ESCALATED/AGED decision entries, not nondecision entries; the actual defect is later VERIFY-GAP/failure interleaving above pending decisions (V1 section 10.2). V1 COV-4 bars all-clear in the default but conditionally permits it elsewhere, so my preference for V3's scope-qualified wording applies across concepts, not just the default (V1 section 3.6; V3 section 10). These corrections agree with both rival Round 2 rechecks.

**NE-5. Local completeness and global census are separate claims.** V3's fixture can define a known one-host scope while G04 leaves real intended inventory unresolved; neither a successful local collection nor exhausted worker paging proves the durable decision census (V3 section 4 closing paragraph; sections 3, 10 and 12 G02/G04). Thus the host criticism is a reason to make the expected-versus-queried set explicit, not a reason to remove the independent `decision census unknown` warning.

These are findings from rereading supplied documents. No external research, collector calls, rendering measurements, client-size checks or participant studies were performed.

## Shared Assumption Responses

- **A-001 — QUALIFY.** Suppression is safe only for positively established routine traffic that contains no unresolved ask; ambiguous traffic must remain visible as candidate attention or explicitly unclassified, not be silently discarded. V3's candidate/uncertain-identity semantics are the closest foundation but do not themselves classify messages or guarantee that visibility (V3 section 3; R06). V2's printed WATCH threshold is useful for liveness exceptions, not classification evidence (V2 sections 2.4 and 4.1). A named classification rule and safe ambiguity behavior remain missing.

- **A-002 — REJECT.** A chat reader is not thereby the acting owner, even if a single designated operator is a valid deployment case. V3 expressly separates requesting worker from acting human and leaves missing ownership unknown; G05 asks for the identity source (V3 section 3 and section 12). V2's responsibility bands are useful presentation, but `w-07 > coord` does not name the answerer, as its Round 2 CR-8 concedes (V2 sections 4.1-4.2). A-002 therefore moves to V3. A heading such as NEEDS YOU must not create assignment, acceptance or acknowledgement.

- **A-003 — QUALIFY.** A line/column target is not a verified platform limit or fence guarantee. V2 most explicitly leaves the client question open and says no chat client was tested (V2 section 12.2, question 7); I retain it as the least assumptive treatment, not the smallest layout. V1 EV-10 is a proposed width check, not an executed total-message-size test (V1 section 14). V3's yielding target cannot bound arbitrary question length (V3 section 4 rule 7). The eventual transport check must preserve the whole mandatory frame/accounting and avoid publishing a truncated successful-looking report.

- **A-004 — REJECT.** Timestamped static pastes do not establish that a reader chose the latest one or that its evidence remains current when read. V3 distinguishes generated time, collection time and evidence time, expressly rejecting fresh-looking reprints of old observations (V3 section 3). That is the strongest existing foundation, but not a reader-side validity boundary. The merged design must require rechecking when an old observation is used for a current action and must not imply that the paste updates itself; none of the original variants supplies that complete rule.
