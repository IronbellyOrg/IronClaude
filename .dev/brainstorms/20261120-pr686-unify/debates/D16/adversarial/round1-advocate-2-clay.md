# D16 Round 1: Advocate for Variant 2 (CLAY, the lightest change)

Written 2026-11-20. Facts re-read today with read-only commands (marked "re-read") or taken from the variant papers and FULL-REPORT.md. UNVERIFIED marks anything I did not read.

## Position summary
Start with the written agreement (roles by login, "silence is never a yes", a reversal line, publish-within-a-day), add a named fallback and a deadline so it cannot stall, and keep one admin setting in reserve with a stated trigger instead of switching it on now. The incident came from an unanswered question, an undefined word ("owner"), and owner decisions nobody could see; one hour of words fixes those causes, while most of the heavy package is days of work that would have passed or missed this particular miss. I concede that the agreement alone has no teeth, and that a narrow path-limited required review is the one heavy item with a good cost-to-value ratio.

## Steelman of Variant 1
The strongest version of Variant 1: this was not a one-off lapse, it was the system working as configured. Master has no protection and no ruleset (re-read: `gh api repos/IronbellyOrg/IronClaude/branches/master/protection` returns 404 "Branch not protected"; `.../rulesets` returns `[]`). Nothing made a second person say yes. PR 681 (1,412 lines added, one file, `docs/designs/orchestrator.md`) merged about 90 minutes after opening, by its author, with no human review other than the author's own six COMMENTED reviews (re-read; `reviewDecision` empty). PR 682 (35 added, 4,599 deleted) merged 66 minutes after opening, by its author, with one bot review. Self-merge is the norm: of the last 60 merged PRs, 29 were `clayphi` merged by `clayphi`, 22 `ironbelly` by `ironbelly`, 8 `clayphi` merged by `ironbelly`, 1 the other way (re-read), so the only gate on any PR is the author's own judgment. A written rule alone repeats 2026-10-22: a rule said in a meeting and never written, and still unwritten (issue 290: the owner decided on 2026-10-29 and handed it to `clayphi`; the 2026-11-17 status review says "nothing has been written down since"; re-read). The only mechanism that turns silence into a block instead of a yes is a server-side setting, and the repository is public (re-read: visibility PUBLIC), so rulesets cost nothing in plan fees. The admin step is about 15 minutes. Against a 59-file, +14,306 / -16,637 line PR 686 sitting open on top of a merged design and a merged deletion, 15 minutes is cheap insurance.

What Variant 1 genuinely gets right: (a) a mechanical path trigger does not depend on the author's own judgment of what counts as a "design"; (b) the decision log answers gap 6, the owner decisions that live in memory files, an unpushed plan and Coder issues 515 and 516; (c) it is the only package that changes the default from "silence allows" to "silence blocks"; (d) it admits its own queue cost.

Issue 655 cited 13 related items and said "replaces #450" (FULL-REPORT.md sections 1 and 8). That is a person who believed they had asked and that they held the "owner" role, not someone who skipped a search. FULL-REPORT.md section 9 reads it the same way. Defining "owner" by login (gap 3) and writing that a question to a named person needs a written answer (U-007) fix that belief directly.
2. **Most of the heavy package would have passed this incident.** A "Related work" section or a stop-on-overlap check (section 11 item 1) would have been filled in: the cited list already existed. The things missed were an unpushed zero-setup plan, Coder issues 515 and 516 and owner decisions in memory files (FULL-REPORT.md section 1 item 3). No search reaches those. Only the owner publishing them does, which is what V2's rule 4 asks for.
3. **The path list is brittle.** I read PR 682's file list (re-read): 36 files, 32 of them outside V1's proposed paths. The biggest deletion, `src/superclaude/cli/issue_pipeline/` (1,597 deleted lines in the package source alone), is not on the list. V2's trigger ("deletes code merged in the last 30 days") would have caught both 682 and 686: PRs 447, 448, 584, 591 were all merged 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-17 (re-read).
4. **It adds no queue where the work is being built.** V1's paths (`issue_watch/`, `docs/designs/`, the sc-issue skill) are the paths of open work right now (PRs 686 and 690, issue 689; V2 fact 8). 7. **It is reversible and measurable.** A paragraph and a template line can be edited in the next PR. An admin ruleset on a public repo is also reversible, but V2 lets the team see whether the words hold before paying the queue cost.
8. **It carries its own tripwire.** See the practical answer below: one self-merged design or deletion after the agreement lands is the stated trigger for the admin setting.

## Weaknesses identified in Variant 2
1. **No enforcement.** Master stays unprotected (re-read). If the agreement is broken, nothing stops the merge, and a 66-minute self-merge of a 4,599-line deletion is cheap to repeat. V2's own Risk list says so.
2. **Silence has no deadline.** "Silence is never a yes" is a sentence. It does not say what the author does after three days of silence, so it stalls the author or invites a quiet workaround. On issue 450 the owner's status review is dated 2026-11-17 and Clay's question 2026-11-18; the owner may simply not have seen it. V2 gives the author no written next step.
3. **The self-classified trigger depends on the author.** "Design PR" is judged by the person who also believed they were the owner. V2 rule 3 ("merged by someone other than its author") helps, but it is again a convention. This is X-003: the convention-without-reviewer pattern is exactly what V1 says failed.
4. **The owner's side is under-covered.** Rule 4 asks for work to be pushed as a draft within a day, but the owner's already-made decisions (Decision 6, Q35 to Q41, Coder 515 and 516) stay where they are. V2 does not say the owner should publish them once. This is the real hole in V2, and gap 6 of FULL-REPORT.md section 10.
5. **The rule does not reach sessions by itself.** Both variants place it in CONTRIBUTING.md and the PR template (A-002). This repository is worked mostly by Claude sessions that read the repo `CLAUDE.md`, not CONTRIBUTING.md. V2 needs the same text in `CLAUDE.md` too.
6. **Its vehicle is stalled.** Issue 290 has been open and assigned to `clayphi` since at least 2026-10-29 with nothing written (re-read, four comments, all from `ironbelly`). Making 290 the vehicle without a date repeats the failure.
7. **My fact 5 (the shared login) is overstated.** See Concessions.
8. **Costs are my estimates and none were measured.**

## Concessions
1. **Repository state.** The repository is public, so the plan-fee argument is not available to me; protection and rulesets are free to switch on. Org-level rulesets could not be read: UNVERIFIED.
2. **The shared-login argument is weaker than my paper made it.** The shared `ironbelly` login means an approval under it does not prove the person gave it. But for the Clay collision the rule needed is "someone other than `clayphi` merged it", and a required approval under any other login does that. The argument only holds if the approver must be a person who read the PR, and no setting can check that. I keep it as a limit on what V1 promises, not as a reason to reject V1.
3. **Convention without a reviewer did fail here.** X-003 is a fair hit on V2 rule 3. My answer is partial: before 2026-11-20 no written convention on owner, design merges or silence existed (CONTRIBUTING.md has no roles text; re-read: its one "maintainers" sentence is about the rot budget at line 77), so what failed was the absence of a rule and the verbal rule of 2026-10-22, not a written one. That is not evidence that a written rule holds. Evidence that written team rules hold in this repository: UNVERIFIED.
3b. **The deletion is on the heavy side of the line.** A merged 4,599-line deletion of the owner's three-day-old code is the worst thing that happened, and a server-side gate is the only thing that stops the next one. I cannot show a written rule would.
4. **V1 is right on the decision log in kind.** I drop it as a folder with a back-fill (2 to 3 hours of agent time, V1's own estimate), but the owner's decisions that open work depends on must be published once. That is U-001's point and V2 lacks it.
5. **The path list catches the incident.** 681 matches `docs/designs/`, 682 matches `WORKER-CONTRACT.md`. Brittle, but it would have stopped both.
6. **V1's 24-hour wait is not something GitHub enforces** (I know of no such rule setting; UNVERIFIED), but V1 does not depend on it; the required review does the work.

## Shared assumptions A-001..A-004
- **A-001 (the owner or an alternate answers within a bounded time): QUALIFY.** True and unstated in both papers. It hurts V1 more than V2: a required code-owner review with one admin as sole owner stalls the PR when the owner is away, and only the admin can set a bypass. In V2 a missed answer produces a delay, not a block, and the fallback (3 working days, then the named alternate) is two sentences. V2 needs that fallback; as written it lacks one (Weakness 2).
- **A-002 (the rule reaches the sessions that do the work): QUALIFY.** The gap is real for V2: put the same four sentences in the repo `CLAUDE.md`, which the sessions read at start (about 10 more minutes). A server-side setting reaches a session only when its merge fails, and a session meeting a block cannot tell whether to ask, wait or retry; a line in `CLAUDE.md` reaches it before it starts. Whether sessions would follow a `CLAUDE.md` line is UNVERIFIED.
- **A-003 (Clay accepts `ironbelly` as owner for design rulings): QUALIFY.** Both variants fail without it; the merged design says "approved by the owner (Clay)" (FULL-REPORT.md section 4) and "owner" appears 83 times in 14 files under `docs/designs/` (re-read count of the phrase "the owner"; I did not check who each one means: UNVERIFIED). The roles paragraph must be written by the owner and accepted in writing by `clayphi` in the PR that adds it. A V1 ruleset applied before that acceptance is an order, and the dispute moves to the merge button. V2 resolves it by agreement first, which is cheaper than a block that settles it by force. I would also let the owner name an area owner for a design in the issue, so "owner" is not a single bottleneck.
- **A-004 (the next collision will look like this one): QUALIFY.** It is true for V2 rule 3 and V1's path list, both of which target design and deletion PRs; it is not true for the silence rule and the publish-within-a-day rule, which do not depend on the shape of the PR. A large feature PR that duplicates open work, like PR 686 itself (14,306 added, 16,637 deleted), is caught only by the visibility rule and by the 30 day window, not by the path list unless it touches the listed paths. Neither variant covers a duplicate feature on unlisted paths; V1's `/sc:overlap` would, but at days of cost and only for what is pushed.

## Points that matter most
**1. Enforcement (C-002, C-005, U-002).** This is where V1 is strongest and V2 weakest. Master is unprotected (re-read), so a convention cannot stop a merge. I do not claim words are enough. I claim the order matters: write the rule and get `clayphi` to accept it in a PR, then see if it holds. If it is broken once, the admin turns on the ruleset on the same day; the setting takes minutes and the CODEOWNERS file can be prepared in advance by any maintainer. That costs one more possible incident of the 681/682 kind against skipping a queue and a dispute over who "the owner" is. Given that PR 686 (59 files) is already open on a merged design, a reasonable owner may say the risk is not worth waiting. If so, the narrow version (two paths, `clayphi` as second code owner) is the one I would accept; I would not accept the five-path list with the owner as sole code owner, because it queues the work now in flight.

**2. The trigger (C-003, X-003).** A mechanical path list sounds safer than a self-classified rule, but PR 682 shows its weakness: of 36 files, 32 were off V1's list, and the 1,597 deleted lines in `src/superclaude/cli/issue_pipeline/` were off the list. It was caught through a 13-line edit to `WORKER-CONTRACT.md`. V2's "deletes code merged in the last 30 days" would have caught 682 and 686, since PRs 447, 448, 584 and 591 were merged between 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-17 (re-read). Its weakness is that the author applies it. I would pair them: the 30-day rule as the written convention, a short path list as the setting, so each covers the other's blind spot.

**3. Silence and the owner's side (C-005, U-001, U-007, A-001).** The incident began when a question to a named person got no written answer. V2's one sentence fixes the direction, but only with a deadline and an alternate, otherwise it stalls the asker. The owner's decisions are the other half: no template or search reaches an unpushed plan or Coder issues 515 and 516 (FULL-REPORT.md section 1 item 3). A one-time publish of the decisions open work depends on does more than a `docs/decisions/` back-fill of 8 to 10 rulings, and V2 must add it. I concede this to V1.

**4. Machinery that contradicts standing rules (C-006, C-010, X-002).** `/sc:overlap` and stop-on-overlap are the expensive items and the least connected to this miss. Issue 655 cited 13 items; the items that were missed were not on GitHub as searchable text. Issue 290 says "not to create new skills or commands yet". Drop both. If a future incident shows a duplicate feature on pushed work, revisit then, with the tripwire.

**5. Cost and the people (C-012, C-013, A-003).** Three maintainers and one admin on a shared login on the owner's host. The cheap items cost about an hour; the heavy items cost days and make the owner a queue. The shared login does not defeat a rule of "not the author", but it does mean the "owner" yes is only as good as the owner reading it. The words can say that the owner's yes is given by a person in a comment, not by a session; whether that is followed is UNVERIFIED. The risk I cannot remove: if the owner and Clay do not agree who the owner is (A-003), neither package works, and a ruleset would make the disagreement a blocked merge button instead of a conversation.
