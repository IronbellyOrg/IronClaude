## 1. Problem Statement

- **The question:** Which ONE unified central Review Panel design (a shared core plus thin modules) captures all the value of E1, E2 and E3 and keeps future PR code review and rf-qa replacement unblocked? E1 = reflect Tier 2 panel kernel (merged as `d2d8a2d16` / #519 on 2026-11-12) with its shadow mode and 15-run model-combination experiment; E2 = the tasklist review-panel study, five collaboration designs and two process-redesign brainstorms on `origin/ironbelly/eval-tasklist-review-panel`; E3 = the #346 review loop (`superclaude review` v1 path, spec-panel Review Loop, loop prompts) plus five installed-only variants (R01 header and legend).
- **Why it matters:** The research files establish seven facts that a unified design has to account for.
  - The three efforts overlap in four places only: the finding id regex and findings-v1 grammar, the shared `cli/review` panel engine, the review cap constant 3, and E2's unchanged use of E3's `review merge` (R01 summary, [CODE-VERIFIED]). They conflict in twelve registered places (R01 section 5, C-1 to C-12): finding location type, blocking rule, fingerprint, ledger schema, convergence threshold, exclusion and fallback location, dispatch, report ending, run record, loop-contract vintage.
  - Two Tier 2 engines exist inside E1 and share only `contract.py` (R06 section 8c): the in-process swarm ensemble behind `superclaude reflect run`, and the prose skill panel (`sc-reflect-protocol`) with its shadow engine. The panel kernel in `cli/review` is wired only to the second (R01 section 2, R06 section 4.1).
  - E3i is a second, stdlib-only implementation of the whole loop that imports nothing from `cli/review` and is not installed or removed by `superclaude install` (R01 section 6, R03 section 3.1, R14 section 3, [CODE-VERIFIED]).
  - E2 changed no file under `src/` or `tests/` at any commit (R01 section 1.1, [CODE-VERIFIED]). It contributes data, a working non-Claude dispatch script, collaboration designs and the only ground truth (16 items in 3 packages) (R01, R08).
  - Evidence of quality is thin. E1 has no ground-truth scoring for any of its 30 runs and a Stage 2 exit that reads `OVERALL: FAIL (shadow runs 13 of 20)` (R06 section 6.1, R07 section 3). E2 has 3 packages, one judge and replicates that reuse the same packages (R08 section 7). E1 and E2 are one evidence family: the E1 input is byte-identical to the E2 designSpec candidate, sha256 `044809e3a963fad395cfbe0988bece975650a3adf17176893665d92338bd3481` (R16 Part 6).
  - The defect register holds 171 rows (170 distinct): 75 belong to the core, 29 to the reflect module, 4 to the tasklist and spec modules (R17 final register).
  - Two future consumers depend on specific core properties. The PR code review module needs file:line locations (the findings-v2 parser accepts them, the ledger path does not), module-declared category, lens and severity sets, an optional per-slot lens, and read-only reviewers; rf-qa replacement needs the same set plus a checklist output mode and a behavioural oracle (R13 sections 7-8, [CODE-VERIFIED]). No product code records the served model (R04 section 4.2).

Probes executed at HEAD (R01 s3, R02 s3-4, [CODE-VERIFIED by execution]): P-a `annotate()` on a citations-only finding raises `KeyError: 'anchor'` (`findings.py:349`), and `ledger.merge_round` calls `annotate` (`ledger.py:136`); mechanism refinement from Gate 1: `merge_round` parses with the v1 `parse_block`, which already requires `anchor`, so a v2 block does not reach `annotate` through that path, and the KeyError arises when a v2-validated citations-only finding is passed to `annotate` directly. P-b a line with `calibrated_confidence` is valid in v1 and invalid in v2. P-c anchor 201, claim 301 and quote 201 characters are each rejected in v2 as in v1. P-d `refs_norm({'anchor':'Task T01.02 does x','claim':'T01.02 bad','refs':['T01.02','AC-0.4','1.2']})` returns `['1.2', 'AC-0.4']`: `T01.01`-style task ids are legal finding ids but are not refs; `_REF_TOKEN` (`findings.py:29`) needs 1-4 capital letters, a hyphen and digits, or `P<digit>`; `telemetry._ID_TOKEN` (`:23`) is a second copy, without the `SHA-` filter. Effect: findings are kept, refs ignored, clustering falls back to proximity; v2 clustering inherits it (`cluster.py:64, 141`). The v2 fingerprint hashes role, path, an empty section and refs, never line numbers, so two findings with the same primary path and the same extractable ref cluster at ANY distance (executed). Anchor-only findings never cluster in v2 (singleton clusters).

**What a dropped line costs (R02 s5, units re-derived).** The 92 / 80 / 49 of the E2 study are finding lines, reviewer-merges and blocking finding lines. Of the 92 lines, 88 (56 + 18 + 14) are length failures that truncation would address, 4 are not (3 bad severity, 1 bad refs; 1 of them blocking); of the 49 blocking lines 48 are length failures. Recovery by re-ask is unmeasured (E2 Spike 5 was not run); keep-and-warn recovers 0. A v2 card that holds one over-length anchor loses the whole reviewer in clustering, whereas the v1 ledger loses one finding. The loss unit therefore depends on which pipeline a finding enters. `card_convert.py` is the only place that truncates (claims to 297 characters plus `...`; `refs` always `[]`; never a quote). Neither brief states every limit: the v1 brief states the 12-200 anchor only (`prompts:60`); the reflect brief (`reviewer-spec.md:39`) states claim and citation limits but not the quote length.
