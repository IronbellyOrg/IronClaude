## 8. The strongest objection and my answer

<!-- Source: Base (original) -->
**Objection.** The metric of record has no power on the data that exists. The pilot shows 0 of 8 real-blocking catches in every arm (E-1) and the replicated default panel shows 0.17 of 8 (`R08:97`). The collaboration design itself says its plan "cannot detect the central estimates of any slot" (`merged-requirements.md:980`, R16:101, [UNVERIFIED by me]). A gate on a quantity that is near zero in every arm cannot rank anything, so Tier Q would return "no evidence" for every candidate, while the any-severity count at least moves (1.83 of 14 for the default panel). A gate that never opens is not an acceptance test.

**Answer.** Power is a property of the data, not of the definition. The any-severity count does move, but E-4 shows why: 89 percent of its catches are three easy defects, so it moves with how often those three are hit and says nothing about whether a blocking defect is caught. The purpose of the gate is a blocking verdict, and the one rule that exists already passes a panel on the any-severity count while no arm finds an item that was blocking in implement (E-1). "No evidence" is the correct output when that is the state of the evidence; a wrong "pass" costs more than a slow "lead". The rule is required to state the minimum detectable effect and the comparison count up front (R16:135, row 4), so "no power at this n" is a reported result, not a failure to report. Tier G adds blocking-in-implement items through the hold-out and the seeded sets, which is where the power comes from; the any-severity count stays in every report as a secondary number so no information is lost. I do not claim the hold-out will reach the needed power; M-3 is the test for it, and a result of "zero in every arm" is a result against this metric as a gate.
