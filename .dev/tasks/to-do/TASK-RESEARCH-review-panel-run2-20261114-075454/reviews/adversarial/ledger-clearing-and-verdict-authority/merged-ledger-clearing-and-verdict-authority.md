## 1. The position in one paragraph

<!-- Source: Base (original, modified) - Section 1 paragraph replaced per Change 1 (quorum gate in place of the quote gate), Change 2 (evidence key record-only), Change 3 (typed authority plus CRITICAL floor), Change 4 (prior lines narrowed, dissent to `regressed`), Change 5 (derived-verdict surface), Change 7 (terminal defer, no expiry), Change 8 (stored keys dropped, text-hash guard) -->
<!-- Source: Variant A, Section 1 and Section 2 clearing, verdict and blocking rows - merged per Changes 1, 3, 5 -->
A finding leaves the loop only on an attributed answer, and silence is not evidence. In a re-review, a row the ledger holds as `resolved_check` (claimed fixed by a fix-map line plus a hunk over its span) becomes `resolved` only when a quorum gate passes: at least `need` distinct reviewers whose findings block is valid answered that row with a `prior` line `resolved`, no reviewer whose block is valid answered it `open` or `regressed`, and no member of the round re-found it (`need = min(Q, requested)`, section 2.1 B1). A row nobody answers stays `resolved_check`, counts as open in the verdict and is listed in the report as "unverified". A valid `open` or `regressed` answer moves a `resolved_check` row to `regressed`; a prior line never moves a row that is not in `resolved_check`. The optional `evidence` quote on a prior line is recorded with a flag that says whether it occurs in the new text; it never gates. The text a round is merged against is bound to the revision output by a stored hash. The `blocking` value stays the reviewer's typed boolean, with one floor applied in `merge_round`: a `CRITICAL` finding is blocking, the typed value is kept as `blocking_typed` and every flip adds a warning; there is no category predicate and no blind grader in the core, and the reviewer's own `verdict:` line stays parser-neutral. The verdict is derived from the rows on every read and is never stored (`brief_ids`, `verify_ids`, `held_ids`). `defer` needs an owner and a reason, has no expiry, never changes a round record and never changes a verdict: a deferred blocking row stays counted as open, and when only deferred rows remain the decision is ESCALATE, not PASS; a re-found deferred row stays deferred and is reported. This is the machine form of what the shipped master loop already states in prose (`spec-panel.md:260,262`): a re-review with no prior table fails, and a deferred finding counts as open. One point is OPEN: when `--resolution` is absent, one advocate holds that the requested reviewers are the reports passed, the other that `need` equals Q (section 2.1 B1). Three HIGH conditions found by the invariant probe are also open (section 4.3).

A "fake-fix" condition in which the reviser's fix map claims `applied` over a span that a hunk touches while the defect remains. Per arm and per round: wrongly cleared rows (defect still present in the revised text), wrongly held rows (defect gone, no answer), prior-line compliance (answers per live blocking row, by ledger id), reviewer output size added by the optional quotes, reviews to a terminal state, escalations at the cap, and added tokens per round. The wrongness rule is registered before the first run, as `DECISIONS.md` D6 did for the #346 experiment (`research/14` 2.3), with at least three replicates per arm because the first-review spread across the ten measured loops ran from 7 to 18 labels (`research/14` 2.2) [UNVERIFIED here]. The `quote_in_doc` flag recorded by B3 is the data that shows whether a quote gate would add power.
- M2. A free pre-measurement on existing data: for the re-review reports of the #346 loops and any E2 re-review report, count how many carry one prior line per live blocking row, by ledger id and not by the presence of prior lines. The shipped brief asks for it (E3). If compliance is low, the unanswered-means-open rule converts into cap escalations and the design is wrong in practice; if compliance is high, the added cost is small. The nearest existing proxy is the prior-findings table completeness in the recorded master-loop re-reviews (ARM B is the PR 427 text, `research/14` section 7.2), not measured here [UNVERIFIED]. A second free proxy: `annotate` already computes `anchor_status` missing/found (`findings.py:350`), and the miss rate of anchors in existing reports is the miscopy rate a quote gate would inherit; not yet measured [debate R2].
- M3. Floor agreement: on every finding corpus that carries typed `severity` and `blocking` (the E2 round-1 ledgers and the #346 variant ledgers), count the flips of the CRITICAL floor (the warning of B6) against the typed value. A high flip count shows that reviewers type CRITICAL findings as non-blocking and that the INV-J endangerment of section 4.2 is real. M3 stays an offline measurement, not a code path.

The converged design is shown wrong if M2 shows prior-line compliance too low for the cap (held rounds reach the cap on fixtures where the control clears correctly), or if M1 shows a false-clear rate that is not clearly below the control's. It is shown to have the wrong strength if the quote-verified arm shows materially fewer false clears at a small per-round cost; the quote is then promoted to a gate, with the deletion carve-out and the span fix of B3. This rule fixes no numbers; they are committed before the first run (the X-14 tier Q discipline).

## 7. The strongest objection and the answer

<!-- Source: Base (original, modified) - Section 7 objection and answer rewritten for the converged design -->
<!-- Source: Variant A, Section 7 (self-report objection and answer 1 and 3) - merged for the residue of the record-only quote -->
Objection. The converged design replaces a behaviour that is pinned as a feature with a behaviour nobody has measured. The only prior implementations are single-reviewer variant scripts with "Convergence not proven; no validated winner" (E14); every E2 ledger is round 1 only; the design adds a reviewer answer per row at a cost no run has priced; and a reviewer who skips the prior lines turns every re-review into an escalation. Worse, with the quote recorded and not gating, the answer is a self-report: a `prior L1 resolved` line costs a reviewer as little as silence costs today, and a reviewer that did not read the revised region types it as readily (INV-013). The design would then move the failure from "silence clears" to "an unverified affirmation clears".

Answer.

- The design does not claim that explicit answers make loops converge; the variants' lack of a valid result is about convergence and about the surrounding machinery (sweeps, audits, models that changed mid-run, `research/14` 6.1), not about this transition rule. The claim is narrower: a row may not leave the `open`-equivalent states on silence. The defect is reproduced at HEAD (E1) and was designed in with a docstring that says the opposite (`ledger.py:112` against `:254-258`).
- The record of an answer removes reproduced paths that need no dishonesty at all: an unrelated empty block clears a row found by 3 of 3 reviewers; a reviewer with an invalid block clears rows (the docstring at `ledger.py:112` says it must not); and, when `--resolution` is passed into `merge_round` (new code in `merge_cmd.py`, E23), a requested reviewer that returned nothing counts for nothing. After the change every cleared row carries a positive, attributable statement by a named reviewer with a valid block and a recorded report hash (`ledger.py:146`); today a cleared row carries no attributable evidence. The residue (a false affirmation) is smaller than the removed set and visible in the history; it is not closed, and the probe says the closure of D-1 holds for the silence path only (INV-013, section 4.3).
- The two failure modes are asymmetric. If the cost estimate is wrong, the result is more rows held and, at worst, an ESCALATE at the 3-review cap with the unanswered rows listed: bounded by `cap_reviews` (`ledger.py:93-96`), visible, and in the direction INV-I names as the repository's priority. If silence-clears is wrong, the result is a defect cleared and a PASS that no later step can see (E1, E4, E5). Without a measurement, the rule whose error is bounded and visible is the safer default.
- A `resolved` answer is not proof of a correct fix, and the design does not say it is. It is an attributed, checkable claim: the reviewer id and model are in the row history, the optional quote is recorded with its `quote_in_doc` flag, and a quote that does not occur is recorded as such (`quote_in_doc` false) and neither clears nor holds a row. The quote was not made a gate because the evidence against it is established in the debate (E20: deletion fixes leave nothing to quote, the replacement span is lossy, and a lazy reviewer can quote any line); M1 measures how much power a gate would add and promotion needs that result (B3).
- The cost is mostly already paid. The shipped re-review brief asks for one prior line per blocking row (E3, `INS_B`); the ledger throws the answers away. The new part is the optional `evidence` string, and the rows that need an answer are the blocking live rows only (E8, E16). Prior-line compliance in existing re-review reports (M2) can be counted before any code is written.
- The pins are a cost of any fix to D-1, not of this design alone: `test_cleared_after_the_next_round_and_refound_stays_open` and `test_one_valid_block_is_enough_and_the_invalid_reviewer_is_reported` assert silence-clears by name (research 03 section 2, I-7).
- What the converged design leaves open, stated so it is not read as claimed: the `--resolution`-absent rule (one side: requested = the reports passed; the other: `need = Q`; OPEN, section 2.1 B1, INV-004); the quorum value Q and the round-1 partial panel (C-03); a MAJOR typed `blocking: false` still passes (INV-016); and the three HIGH conditions INV-001, INV-002 and INV-003 (section 4.3). A change in the meaning of `verdict` for stored `review-ledger/1` files is an INV-D question that the design does not settle (section 4.2).
