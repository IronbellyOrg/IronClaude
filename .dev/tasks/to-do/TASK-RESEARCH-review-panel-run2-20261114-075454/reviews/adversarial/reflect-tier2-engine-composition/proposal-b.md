## 1. The position in one paragraph

Choose by subtraction or by contract, not by building a hybrid. The two Tier-2 engines are two producers of one verdict contract. They are reconciled at that contract, which is the one thing they already share (`contract.parse_contract` and `contract.derive_verdict`, r06 s8(c)), and not by putting engine B's data model inside engine A's execution shell. In practice: (i) the decided vocabulary fixes are made (CV-1 threshold 0.80, CV-2 single vendor degrades, CV-3 one diversity word, CV-5 `merge_method: adversarial` means a disk-confirmed merge, CV-6 the `reviewer_briefs_materialized` ref line is the contradicting side, CV-8 the documents stop claiming an executor-disjoint ensemble, CV-9 an empty baseline is "no baseline"); (ii) the deterministic shadow steps (RE-2, decided) move into code as small `superclaude review` helpers that the skill's existing CLI call sites invoke, inside engine B's own boundary, so `cli/reflect` `runner.py`, `ensemble.py`, `fallback.py`, `_diversity.py` and `contract.py` keep their behaviour; (iii) engine A stays the production Tier-2 gate of `superclaude reflect run` and is described honestly as a convergence-and-diversity gate that performs no deviation audit; (iv) no capability moves between the engines now. If the owner (r06 gap 1, GQ-067: "does the wrapper switch to engine B, or does engine A gain exclusion?") or a quality measurement later requires exactly one engine, the position names its choice: retire engine A's reviewer path and let the wrapper's existing Tier-1 child launch run the skill panel (synth-05 Option 3), because giving engine A a tool loop, a calibrator, an evidence validator and the four deviation classes (Option 2) is the XL path through plain-completion transports. That later step is a gate with a stated trigger (section 6), not part of the design now.
