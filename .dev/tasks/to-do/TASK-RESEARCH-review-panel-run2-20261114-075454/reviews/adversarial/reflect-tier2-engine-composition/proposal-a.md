## 1. The position in one paragraph

Reflect keeps one Tier-2 review path with two reviewer lanes behind it, and the two engines are composed by role instead of chosen between. The shell that runs and judges a Tier-2 audit stays engine A's: `ReflectRunner` (`src/superclaude/cli/reflect/runner.py`), the pure `derive_verdict` map with exit codes 0/10/11/2, the atomic write-back, the recursion marker, the reviewer-isolation snapshot, the bounded fallback ladder (`fallback.py`) and the swarm transport (live and `stub`). The data that flows through the shell is engine B's: contract 1.8.0 (110 stable plus 27 telemetry names), the findings-v2 block and the cluster / verify-citations / manifest / panel-resolve kernel in `src/superclaude/cli/review/`, the reviewer card format, `merged-verdict.yaml` and the four-class deviation taxonomy. Every step that today is an LLM copying, naming or laying out files (resolve-and-freeze, the parity record, card headers and file names, the contract file, `merged-verdict.yaml`, the key-set check, the first-pass compliance number, `kept-ids`) becomes a code step that the orchestrator calls through a `superclaude` subcommand. The LLM keeps what only an LLM can do: reviewing, deviation classification, blind calibration scoring, the debate and the evidence validator's re-read. Neither engine is retired, neither verdict map nor contract version is replaced, and no capability listed in section 4 is dropped.
