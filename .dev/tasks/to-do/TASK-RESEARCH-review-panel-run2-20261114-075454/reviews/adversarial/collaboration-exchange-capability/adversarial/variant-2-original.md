## 1. The position

The product should own one small, dormant exchange transport now: the hook skeleton that four E2 packages wrote four times (baked arguments, always exit 0, subagent-call skip, `flock` counter, finish signal, transcript seal that fails closed while the review fails open, exact JSON `additionalContext` output), a board outside the reviewed tree written only by the harness, the `panel-msg/1` validator with a per-kind key table and forbidden verdict keys, and the between-phase (F1, `--resume`) exchange as the one family that is expressible in more than one runtime. It is off by default and its off state is a launch command that differs from the isolated panel by nothing, pinned by an argv-diff test. The static kit and H1/H2 ship as well; nothing in B removes them. B does not claim that exchange improves what reviewers find: nobody has measured that, and the E2 plan states it cannot detect the central gain (MR near `:1128`). B claims four narrower things. (1) The decisive measurement that the other position waits for (an exchange arm against the static kit on real ground truth) can only be run by someone other than the study's producer if a transport exists in the product. (2) The skeleton already exists four times and the copies have already diverged, so one tested copy is cheaper than four. (3) The transport is where the open runtime questions become answerable (gateway ids, subagent delivery, read-only receivers), because the core owns the launcher. (4) With the switch off the cost is zero by construction and testable. B's boundary: harness-authored message kinds only (F1 digest, F3 move, F6 coverage); the model-authored writers (F4 sweeper and F7 flagger through a Bash helper) are not in the core, because the read-only `reflect-reviewer` cannot be one and the board reader does not care who wrote a line.

## 8. The strongest objection and the answer

The only measured treatment cost is the kit's; the E2 text says the kit may beat every exchange design; the post-baseline data show the exchange arms often deliver nothing (`k = 0`, late facts) and the kit rows ahead of the isolated rows at small n. A dormant transport still has to be maintained, still adds a launch path and a hook to test, and reaches neither of HEAD's two main runtimes: the swarm HTTP engine has no tool loop and the Agent-tool reviewer is skipped by the skeleton's own subagent filter. Waiting costs nothing because the study can keep running in `.dev/`.

Answer:
- Bounded cost: off state is byte-identical and tested (test 1), the package is engine-local, nothing in reflect, ledger or parsers changes. B does not ask anyone to believe exchange works.
- The wait is not free for the measurement. The trigger "an arm beats the kit on a package with real ground truth" needs the arm to run somewhere reproducible. At the pinned tip no design could run from the commit (section 3.D); at `c44c98a77` three can and r2 cannot [POST-BASELINE], all from a `.dev/eval-workspaces` tree with study-specific paths. A product transport turns the arms into runs anyone can repeat on product panels and puts the served-model and delivered counts in one record, which is the evidence the trigger needs.
