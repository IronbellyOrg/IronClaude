## 1. CLA1 (unambiguous language: no "should consider", "might", "as appropriate")

CLA1 | Variant B | NOT MET | MET | MET | CLAIM: Variant B contains no hedge language of the kind the criterion names, and its decisive rule is concrete. EVIDENCE: Grep of variant-2-original.md for `should consider|might|as appropriate|may|perhaps|possibly|probably|could|likely|somewhat|roughly` returns zero matches. The only hits for a wider pattern are "whether" at lines 34, 35, 75, 109, 116 and "small share" at line 93. Each "whether" is a statement that a question is open or is somebody's decision, for example line 34 "Whether a module's failing verdict blocks anything is the module's choice" and line 116 "whether it is worth the maintenance is the product decision this slot calls it". These are declarations of who decides, not softeners on a claim. The load-bearing success rule at Sec 6 item 3 (line 95) is concrete: "B is shown wrong if arm D's wrong blockers drive a majority of packages to ESCALATE or to a patch on a non-defect, or if arm D's real-blocking catch is below arm R's". Unknowns are tagged `[UNVERIFIED]` or `[CODE-VERIFIED]` throughout (header, line 5), which is the opposite of hedging. REASONING: Pass 1 NOT MET rests on four items. (a) "a small share" at Sec 6 item 1 is a real vague quantifier, but it is one phrase on a free pre-check, not a hedge, and the criterion's examples are hedge phrases. (b) "the module's choice" and (c) "the product decision" are assignments of ownership, not ambiguity. (d) `[UNVERIFIED]` tags are explicit epistemic labels, and penalising them would punish labelling an unknown. Pass 2 is right that the language is direct. The one loose phrase (line 93) and the missing number on the cost decision (line 96, "about 64k tokens per package run" is given as a figure but no decision threshold) are noted as minor wording weaknesses, not a failure of "unambiguous language" as the criterion is written. Note on consistency: Variant A's CLA1 (NOT MET in both passes, not disputed, not re-scored here) rests on its primary success rule at Sec 6.1 ("materially more blocking GT items per cost", line 73) and Sec 6.2 ("If that share is high", line 74), where the main wrong/right test has no threshold. Variant B's main test (line 95) has one. That difference in the load-bearing rule is why the two can differ.

## 2. INV4 (covers count-divergence scenarios: off-by-one, inclusive/exclusive ranges, counts of readers/pins/phases)

INV4 | Variant A | MET (weakly) | NOT MET | NOT MET | CLAIM: Variant A lists counts but does not analyse any scenario where two counts diverge or a boundary is off, so the criterion is not covered. EVIDENCE: Grep of variant-1-original.md for `off-by|inclusive|exclusive|boundary` returns zero matches. The counts it does contain are: "36 `def test_`" (line 33, a size of the existing test matrix); "four" readers of `reflect_status` (line 60, Sec 5 INV-M, and the reader list in Sec 3 item 3, line 31); depth band "0-3" (lines 35, 88); and "2 passes" against the "3-review cap" (line 86). None is examined for divergence. REASONING: Pass 1 credits line 86 ("Stage 7-10 patch loop ... its own cap of 2 passes ... Reconciling a third loop with the 3-review cap and those fences is the larger change"). That sentence uses the 2-versus-3 cap difference as an argument against a competing design (a direct call adding a third loop). It does not state a divergence scenario for Variant A's own position, say what breaks when the counts differ, or give a check. Variant B by contrast turns the same fact into a named risk with an open question for its own design: Sec 5 INV-J (line 75, "'2 TOTAL passes' ... whether that equals 2 fix cycles is the open OQ-24") and Sec 2 C-03 row (line 19, "`requested` can collapse to 1"). The reader count is the one place where Variant A's own count could have diverged, and it did: the fault-finder (invariant-probe.md INV-008) shows more than four kinds of `reflect_status` reader exist, and Variant A never tests its own "four". The depth boundary in line 35 ("0-3 -> `--depth quick --tier 1`") is stated but its edge (score 3 versus 4, the forced-deep `n_cpo >= 1 OR n_strict >= 2` boundary, probe INV-006) is never examined. Pass 1 itself labelled its MET "weak", and the rubric requires a specific citation that covers the criterion, not an adjacent mention. This was a close call; a mention of two differing caps is the only thing that could support MET, and it does not meet the "covers scenarios" wording.

## 3. STR3 (clear separation of concerns between sections)

STR3 | Variant A | NOT MET | MET | MET | CLAIM: Variant A's sections each carry one concern and do not overlap in purpose, which is what "separation of concerns between sections" asks. EVIDENCE: Section purposes are distinct and self-labelled: Sec 1 "The position in one paragraph" (line 9); Sec 2 "Design consequence for the shared core and the named modules" (line 13, a module-by-module table); Sec 3 "Evidence" (line 27, numbered [CODE-VERIFIED] items); Sec 4 "Consistency with the adjudicated slots C-01, C-02, C-03" (line 42); Sec 5 "Invariants it preserves and any it endangers" (line 52, one table); Sec 6 "The test or measurement that would show it right or wrong" (line 71); Sec 7 "Effect on future PR code review and rf-qa replacement" (line 78); Sec 8 "The strongest objection and the answer" (line 82); Sec 9 "Open items this position does not settle" (line 94). Design claims sit in Sec 2, facts in Sec 3, invariant verdicts in Sec 5, tests in Sec 6, with no section doing another's job (Sec 2 states consequences and cites back to Sec 3 rather than re-arguing evidence). REASONING: Pass 1 NOT MET argues that Sec 3 interleaves entry (item 1), effect (item 2), delivery (item 9) and the DO4 decision (item 10) in one list, and that debate point S-002 went to Variant B. That is a criticism of the order inside one section and of not labelling the three parts. Variant B's evidence section (Sec 4, E1-E13) interleaves the same three parts in the same way (E4-E7 entry, E2-E3 effect, E9 delivery); B gets its three-part separation only from the single mapping in Sec 8 item 4 (line 112) and from the bullets of Sec 3. A flat evidence section is a normal and intended shape for a position paper, and Pass 1 scored Variant B's equivalent MET. The debate outcome on S-002 is about whether the three parts are separable in the argument, which both advocates settled in B's favour; that is a content verdict, not a section-structure verdict. Pass 1 also scored Variant A's CLA3 ("each section has one clear purpose") MET with Sec 1, 4, 7 and 9 as evidence; holding STR3 NOT MET at the same time would contradict that. Minor blemishes noted but not disqualifying: Sec 7 point 2 (plugin gap, line 80) and Sec 2's rf-qa and PR review row (line 23) repeat each other, and Sec 4 ends with C-04/C-15 notes (line 50) that are not part of C-01 to C-03.
