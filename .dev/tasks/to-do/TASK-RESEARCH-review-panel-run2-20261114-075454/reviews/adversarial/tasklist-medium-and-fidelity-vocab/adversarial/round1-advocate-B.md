## Position Summary

Keep Medium as a blocking label in the tasklist skill and keep the fidelity prompt as a separate gate. After re-reading the code I rest this on S1's definition of blocking (a missing or incomplete roadmap requirement), not on S4, and I accept one tightening: define Medium as "incomplete or weakened against a stated roadmap requirement" and move pure wording or ambiguity findings to Low. That keeps every shipped and pinned sentence (SK, `src/superclaude/commands/tasklist.md:113`, 12 sweep rows, 2 worker-contract pins) true, and it keeps the High/Medium grade boundary out of the verdict, which matters because Stage 7 is re-sampled by fresh agents on every pass.

## Steelman of Variant A

The strongest A: the repo has one review rule. `pragmatic.md:225-226` (S1, S2) says only blocking findings fail a review and minor ones never trigger a fix pass; `:230` says Medium is a warning that never triggers a fix pass and never enters a reviser brief; `:229` says grade "by the definition above, not by the label" [READ]. The tasklist skill lists `High | Medium | Low` at `SK:1386` and defines none of them [READ], so its "High and Medium (blocking)" text is a label rule, the exact thing S5 was written against. The only written definition in the product is the CLI's (`PR:49-69`), and the CLI gate passes a Medium-only report (`tests/tasklist/test_tasklist_fidelity.py:208-221`, [READ]) and exits 1 on HIGH only (`src/superclaude/cli/tasklist/commands.py:181-183`, [READ]). So A makes the skill agree with two sources, adds the missing grading block, regrades omissions to High so the E2-style harm (a finding class routed to Medium) cannot recur, and states its own costs (4.2 items 1-6) instead of hiding them.

What A gets right: (a) B's "S4 permits it" premise only holds after B's own edit (X-002); (b) B's E7 counterfactual ignores A's regrade (X-004); (c) the skill really has no Medium definition today; (d) A's static tests T1-T3 need no model call and are good.

## Weaknesses Identified in Variant A

The CLI MEDIUM definition lists "Acceptance criteria are weaker than the roadmap's success criteria" as its own example (`PR:62`) [READ]. "Weakened criteria" is one of Stage 7's five dimensions and part of its stated purpose (`SK:1349, 1381`) [READ]. A's answer 2 escapes by saying a weakened criterion that "makes a roadmap-stated criterion unverifiable is High", which is not the CLI text, so the "one definition in the product" argument and the parity test T2 no longer hold on the exact class at stake.
2. **A's boundary mitigation does not exist at Stage 7.** A cites C-03's any-reviewer union (A 4.2 item 4). `SK:1351-1368` gives each task slice to exactly one agent [READ]; there is no reviewer cluster to take a union over (X-009).
