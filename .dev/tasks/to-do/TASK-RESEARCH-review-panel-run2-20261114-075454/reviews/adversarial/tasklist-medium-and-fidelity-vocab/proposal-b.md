## 1. The position

Position B: the tasklist module keeps Medium as a blocking severity (the tasklist skill's own model, which pragmatic.md S4 permits), and the roadmap-to-tasklist fidelity prompt stays a separate gate with its own HIGH, MEDIUM and LOW vocabulary, outside the shared core. The two tasklist uses of the word are two scales for two jobs: in the skill, High and Medium are the two defect labels the patch loop must fix and Low is a note; in the CLI gate, HIGH alone decides exit 1 and MEDIUM is a counted report field. The shared core does not decide this: it carries only the boolean `blocking` (already decided in C-S3, C-01 and C-02), and each module declares, as its own data, how its labels map onto `blocking`. What B adds to that principle for tasklist is that the module's map is `High -> blocking`, `Medium -> blocking`, `Low -> not blocking`, kept where it is shipped and pinned today, rather than being re-pointed at the canonical Medium of S1-S5 (a non-blocking warning among minor findings). The fidelity prompt is not made a core module because its vocabulary, ids, frontmatter and exit code belong to a roadmap-pipeline gate that two other gates share.

## 8. The strongest objection and my answer

Objection (the strongest form of the other side): the repo has one canonical review rule, S1-S5, copied into ten primitives and pinned by tests, and the rule says Medium is a non-blocking warning; the tasklist CLI already behaves that way; so the tasklist skill is the outlier, and its Medium-as-blocking is the same label-driven grading that S5 and the repo owner tried to remove ("grade by the definition, not by the label"). Keeping it leaves two incompatible Mediums inside one product and the core inheriting the confusion. S4 is for protocols with a declared model such as `/sc:implement`, which states its model in its own refs; the tasklist skill states none.

Answer: the objection is right that the skill states no model and no definition of Medium (E4, E6), and B adds both. It is not right that the skill grades by label. The tasklist's finding labelled Medium in the only reports in the repo is mostly an omitted or unmatched roadmap requirement (E6), which is S1's "missing required deliverable"; the word Medium there is a name collision with the S1-S5 sub-tier of minor findings, not an instance of it. The CLI's non-gating Medium is explained by it having no fixer (E5) and by a differently calibrated HIGH (E6), so aligning the skill to it is aligning two things that were calibrated apart. The flip is not free: it rewrites the 12 pinned tasklist rows and the P2 text (E3) and, on the two reports that exist, it would let both bundles ship with all their Medium findings unpatched (E7). Where the answer stops: if M-X15-1 finds that most Medium findings are wording or ambiguity, then the skill's Medium is label-graded in practice, the objection stands, and the right move is to redefine Medium (and possibly demote it), which this proposal does not rule out and does not pre-judge.
