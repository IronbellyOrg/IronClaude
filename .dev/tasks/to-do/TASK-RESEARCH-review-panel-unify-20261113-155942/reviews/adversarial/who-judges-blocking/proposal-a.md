# Proposal A: Single-reviewer veto, with its inputs hardened, for C05

Conflict: C05, who decides blocking (slug `who-judges-blocking`). Side: A. Option: Option A of the C05 block of `synthesis/synth-04-options-conflicts.md` (lines 292-383), the single-reviewer veto that master and `cluster_v2` already implement. Paths starting with `research/` or `synthesis/` sit under `.dev/tasks/to-do/TASK-RESEARCH-review-panel-unify-20261113-155942/`; other paths are relative to the repository root.

## Design

**The decision.** Reviewers decide blocking, each for its own findings, under a per-lens predicate. The core combines their answers with one rule: a cluster (and a ledger row) blocks when any member's effective blocking flag is true, and the verdict is FAIL while one blocking row is open. No second agent, score threshold, vote count or debate sits between the reviewers' flags and the verdict. This is what `ledger.py:187,213-218,274` and `cluster.py:375` already do.

**What changes is the input to the veto, not the veto.** Option A as written in the synthesis is "XS, no files affected". This proposal keeps that rule and says plainly that it is only defensible together with four fixes that other conflicts already decide. They act on what enters the veto, so none of them is a decider:

1. Evidence gate before blocking (C02, D5): a finding whose anchor or citation does not resolve is kept and flagged but cannot open a blocking row until a deterministic re-read confirms it. This is code, not an opinion.
2. Per-lens predicate as data (C16 Option B): the reviewer's flag is an input, the core records an effective flag, `channel: advisory` and `grounding-gap` can never block, and reflect-post derives it from `deviation_class`.
3. Partial reviewers abstain (C15 Option A, D2): a reviewer with rejected lines is `partial`, never clears a row, and never counts as "found nothing".
4. Complete-link clustering (C12): so one blocking member does not turn a 19-member chained row into one mega blocker.

**Asymmetric verdict: one reviewer can fail a plan, two valid reviewers are needed to pass it.** A first review with fewer than two valid reviewer blocks may still return FAIL (the veto), but a would-be PASS is recorded as degraded and does not certify the plan. This resolves D3 without a blocking quorum and matches reflect's own rule that a single-reviewer merge is degraded (`contract.py:295-296`). The veto is about who may stop a plan; the degrade rule is about who may certify one. Degrading, not refusing, keeps spec-panel's one-reviewer fallback usable (`src/superclaude/rules/prompts/document_review_loop_prompts.md:70`, `research/09-consumer-plugpoints.md` §8 item 7 [DOC]).

**Record who and how many, decide nothing with it.** Each cluster already carries `reviewers`, `blocking_any`, `blocking_all`, `support` and `disputed` (`cluster.py:333-377`), and each ledger row carries `confidence: overlap|single` (`ledger.py:86-90`). The core adds one derived count, `singleton_blockers` (blocking clusters whose blocking members all come from one reviewer), to the cluster output and to the telemetry group of C07. Nothing routes on it. It is the measurement a later decider would need (see Risks).

**Cap the panel at three finders.** The only wrong-blocker effect that survived E2's statistics belongs to a four-reviewer panel built from two doubled pairs (E-6, E-7). C14 already prefers a cap of 3 and C04 makes replicas a declared choice. Option A relies on both and does not decide them.

## How it works

**Data flow, first review.**

1. Reviewers (at most three finders; ids distinct unless a replica is declared) write a findings block under the lens brief. Each line carries the reviewer's own `blocking` boolean. The parser already rejects a MINOR finding that blocks (`findings_v2.py:107-108`, `findings.py:100-101`).
2. Parse per line (C15): valid lines are kept, rejected lines are persisted with id and reason, the reviewer is marked `partial` if any line was rejected.
3. Evidence gate (deterministic): anchors and citations are re-read against the snapshot. An unresolved anchor sets `anchor_status: missing` and the finding cannot be effective-blocking until resolved.
4. Effective flag per finding from the lens predicate: spec uses the reviewer's flag after the consequence sentence check; reflect-post uses `deviation_class` and `channel`; advisory and grounding-gap findings are never blocking (grounding gaps route through `needs_human_decision`, as `contract.py:373-374` already treats it).
5. Cluster (complete-link). `blocking_any = any(effective flags)`; `reviewers`, `blocking_all`, `support`, `disputed`, `chained` are computed as today and not consulted for the verdict.
6. Ledger row or reflect shadow record: `row.blocking = cluster.blocking_any` (`ledger.py:187`). Verdict: FAIL if any blocking row is open (`ledger.py:274`); a would-be PASS with fewer than two valid blocks is returned with `degraded: true` and is not a certified PASS.
7. Telemetry: `singleton_blockers`, `blocking_clusters`, rejected-line list, `partial` reviewers.

**Later rounds.** A re-found row takes its blocking flag from the current round's members, as today (`ledger.py:213-218`). Whether later rounds re-find or only verify is C13. A does not touch it, and the D2 fix (an invalid or partial reviewer never clears a row) is the only change to clearing that A depends on.

| `confidence: overlap/single` | ledger row | existing, display only |
| `contract_version`, `merge_method`, every stable reflect key | reflect `return-contract.yaml` | unchanged (1.8.0); panel-only values live in the nested `telemetry:` block that lint L2 does not inspect |

**CLI surface.** No new subcommand and no new required flag. `superclaude review merge`, `review ledger verdict` (exit 0 PASS, 10 REVISE, 11 ESCALATE, `ledger_cmd.py:13`) and `review cluster` keep their shapes. Additions: `singleton_blockers` in the JSON and text output of `review cluster` and `review merge`; the degraded marker for a would-be PASS on fewer than two valid blocks, set inside `merge_round` (whether it keeps exit 0 or gets its own code follows the verdict vocabulary of C19).

## Correctness argument

Each step names the evidence it rests on. Steps 1-7 argue that the veto is the right rule to keep. Step 8 says what this argument does not establish.

1. **The veto is the recall ceiling of every option on the table.** The synthesis's own recommended resolution keeps the any-reviewer set as the wide candidate list and adds a decider on top (`synthesis/synth-04-options-conflicts.md:380`). A decider that filters that list can only remove blockers: for the same inputs, its FAIL set is a subset of the veto's FAIL set. A filter helps only if what it removes is mostly wrong. Measured recall of real blocking defects is already 0.17 to 1.20 of 8 per run for replicated panels and 0 of 8 in the pilot (E-4), so there is little recall to spend, and the only filter rates in the record say filters are weak: true-negative rate below 25% for LLM validators (E-11, external), a calibrator whose numbers are one per card and 0 of 689 per finding in legacy runs (E-10), and a grader nobody has measured (E-11). A quorum filter has a measured hazard instead: it deletes real singletons (E-9). This step cuts both ways: if the veto's FAILs are mostly unrelated to real blocking defects, a filter that removes them costs little recall. So the case for keeping the veto rests on step 2 (the verdict level), on the weakness of every available filter, and on the singleton evidence, not on recall alone.

2. **Read the E2 numbers at the verdict level, where the gate acts.** The 65 versus 38 gap counts ground-truth defects that were flagged blocking and did not block implementation (E-3). That is a severity over-grade on a real defect. It is not an invented defect. A wrong-blocker row on a defect-rich plan still sits on a plan with 4 to 10 known defects, so the plan's FAIL is the right verdict. The harm of a wrong blocker lands on the control package (0.25-1.75 per run, E-2), where it produces an unneeded revision round. The per-run FAIL rate on the control package is not tabulated in the files I read, so I do not claim a number for it. It is the first thing the benchmark must report (see Test coverage).

3. **A veto is monotone and explainable; the alternatives are neither by construction.** Within one round, adding a reviewer, or fixing a defect that dropped a valid finding, can only add blocking rows. Every FAIL traces to a named finding with a quote and a span, and the Reviser brief is the list of those rows (`ledger.py:99-104`, `:274` [CODE]); the replay found 0 inconsistencies in 264 merges (E-1). Under a quorum, a weighted threshold or a grader, a defective plan can pass because its one blocking finding was demoted by a rule that no ground truth supports (E-8, E-10).

4. **The costs of the two errors are unequal in this repository.** A false FAIL becomes REVISE below the cap and ESCALATE at it, and two of the three tasklist review points are advisory (E-14). A false PASS ships a plan, and the single-reviewer pilot arm did exactly that on both defect-rich packages (E-4). The one blocking tasklist gate in production cannot currently fail on drift or regression (E-14). The veto errs toward the error the system can recover from.

5. **The PASS side is corroborated, so the veto does not contradict reflect.** A single-reviewer merge is degraded, never PASS (`contract.py:295-296`), and so is a convergence score below 0.80 (`contract.py:317-326`) [CODE]. The con listed for Option A in the synthesis, that it contradicts reflect's own rule that a single-reviewer outcome is degraded, holds only if the veto also certified. Under this proposal it does not: one reviewer can fail, two valid reviewers are needed to pass (E-13).

6. **Input hardening is where the measured precision loss comes from, and each fix is already decided elsewhere.** Dropped blocking findings (D1), null-span blocking rows (D5), mega-rows (D6) and source-blind reviewers all distorted E2's rows (E-5). The veto is a pure function of effective flags, so improving the flags improves the veto without changing it. The per-lens predicate (C16) also removes the two cases where the code lets the wrong thing block: `channel: advisory` and `grounding-gap` findings may carry `blocking: true` today (`research/05-blocking-calibration.md` §7, [DOC]).

7. **It is the only option that leaves every pin and contract alone.** No new `merge_method`, no stable-field change, no lint change (E-16), and the existing veto pins stay green (E-17). Options B, C and D change ledger and cluster decision code and tests; D also needs a new agent promoted into `src/`.

8. **What this argument does not establish.** It does not show that the veto's precision is acceptable. E-2 is the only ground-truth evidence on any decider, and it is unfavourable on precision. Steps 2 and 6 explain why the numbers overstate the veto's fault; they do not measure how much. The claim that hardening the inputs lowers wrong blockers enough is a prediction. It is falsifiable with data the repository already owns: rerun the three E2 packages (16 ground-truth defects) with the hardened core and compare per-run verdicts and wrong blockers with the pre-fix figures (E-2). If control-package wrong blockers do not fall below an owner-set tolerance, the next step is a filter on `singleton_blockers` only (see Risks), which keeps the veto for every corroborated cluster.

## Risks and mitigations

**1. Weakest point: the precision claim is a prediction, and the only measured precision is bad.** The only ground truth on any decider says the veto over-blocks (E-2). This proposal answers that with input fixes and a panel cap, and nobody has measured their effect. The cap alone cannot carry the argument: the two-reviewer panel `ember + luna` already shows 2.00 wrong blockers per run on the defect-rich packages (CR:317), while `ember + sonnet` shows 0.25 (CR:315), so composition and reviewer behaviour matter as much as count. If the hardened core still leaves control-package wrong blockers above an owner-set tolerance (pre-fix baseline for the default panel: 0.83 per run, CR:331; other replicated panels up to 1.75, CR:315-333), Option A has failed on its own terms. *Mitigation:* run that benchmark before the unified core is declared done (Test coverage, item 9); keep `singleton_blockers` so the next step is cheap and narrow: put a verification filter (a deterministic evidence re-read first, then a blind grader or calibrator if one has been measured) on singleton blockers only. Corroborated clusters keep the veto, and singleton handling is where the external sources disagree (Cursor drops them, LinkedIn verifies them, Zenodo says most true defects are singletons; E-9, E-15).

**2. The spec lens still lets the reviewer grade its own findings.** The consequence sentence in `src/superclaude/commands/spec-panel.md:259` is applied by each reviewer to its own finding (`research/05-blocking-calibration.md` §10.1 [DOC]), and the 27 over-graded catches in E-3 came from that regime. C16 makes reflect-post derive blocking from `deviation_class` in code, which removes self-grading there. *Mitigation:* record `blocking` (reviewer) next to `blocking_effective` (core) per finding and report their disagreement rate in telemetry; keep the brief's "write the sentence" requirement (`document_review_loop_prompts.md:31`, [DOC]).

**3. One reviewer can also clear a row.** A re-found row takes the current round's flag, so one non-blocking re-report un-blocks it (`ledger.py:216-218` [CODE]; pinned by `test_review_ledger.py:518-544`). Under a veto this is the same single-reviewer power on the other side. *Mitigation:* D2 (an invalid or partial reviewer never clears a row) is a prerequisite; whether later rounds re-find or only verify is C13 and A works with either. Under the verify-only protocol clearing needs an evidenced `resolved` answer, which closes this gap.

**4. A future promotion of the veto into reflect's verdict would halt on any one reviewer's Drift.** Reflect halts on any Drift or Regression count (`contract.py:377-381` [CODE]), so a single reviewer's drift finding would halt if the debate were replaced. Two owner decisions are open: whether MINOR drift halts, and who sets `unauthorized_deviation_present` (`research/05-blocking-calibration.md` §13 [DOC]). *Mitigation:* this proposal does not promote anything. The debate stays (C11), the panel veto stays shadow telemetry, and promotion waits for the benchmark in Risk 1 and a contract bump decided under C11.

**5. A panel of different models is not a panel of independent votes.** [external] about two effective votes from nine judges (E-9). The veto does not depend on independence for its recall, but extra reviewers may add correlated wrong blockers. *Mitigation:* cap at three finders, declare replicas (C04), and report each reviewer's blockers that no other reviewer shares, so the panel can be compared with its best single reviewer.

**6. The evidence is thin on both sides.** Three packages, 16 ground-truth defects, n of 4 to 6 per panel, one gateway on 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-13, one judge pass per row whose variance was not measured (CR "Stated limits" [E2@4a22d74]), a judge that saw the panel name (E-3), and ground truth that scores a novel real find as a wrong blocker (PR "Stated limits"). This cuts against every claim in this file, including the ones favouring A. *Mitigation:* C20's portable benchmark with panel-blind labels and a source-aware judge brief.

**7. The PASS rule adds degraded outcomes.** A round with fewer than two valid blocks can no longer return a certified PASS. E2 recorded 80 reviewer-level invalid-block cases under the v1 rule (AD:7 [E2@4a22d74]). Three existing tests assert a one-reviewer round-1 PASS (Test coverage, item 3). *Mitigation:* C15's per-line policy keeps valid lines, so a reviewer with one bad line still has a valid block; the outcome is marked degraded and can be re-run, and the one-reviewer fallback keeps working.

**8. Chained clusters could carry a blocker.** If complete-link clustering (C12) leaves chains, one blocking member would colour a large cluster. *Mitigation:* `chained` is already reported (`cluster.py` docstring [CODE]); C12's acceptance metric covers it.
