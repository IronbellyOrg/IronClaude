## 7. Recommendation

**Recommended approach:** Option B from 6.X Overall design options (a new superset core with pluggable lens, category and location namespaces, and thin per-consumer modules), built *through* Option A's code: findings-v2 becomes the single contract with findings-v1 accepted as a strict subset (C-01), and the closed tuples (`LENSES`, `CATEGORIES`, severity words, `_KINDS`, `STRATA`) move into data registries only as each consumer needs them. Option C (keep both stacks and add adapters) is rejected. This is exactly the "Recommended design (not a roadmap)" of synth-04 6.X; nothing below overrides it.

**Rationale:**

1. **The Options Comparison favours B for the two consumers that matter after the first ones.** On the Options Comparison criteria B is XL effort and Medium-High risk but the only option that is "Strong" for both PR code review and the rf-qa replacement (A is "Weak", C "Weak to medium"), because the future consumers need open lenses, per-lens categories and severities, a location union, multi-artifact candidates, roles separate from models and fix/verdict contracts as data (X1-X13, `research/10` section 5). Option A alone leaves every new lens a code change in closed tuples (C-01 Cons; `findings_v2.py:36`, `findings.py:15-22`). Option C leaves D1, D5, D9, the two opposite invalid-block policies and the third reflect merge path (`ensemble.py`) in place and repeats the severity-word guesses that already invert rf-qa `IMPORTANT` under `card_convert` (`card_convert.py:71-83`).
2. **A is B's first stage, so the order costs nothing.** B starts from A's code (v2 is the natural base, `research/01` section 18; `PROFILES` and `PRIMARY_ROLES` are the registry precedents, `review_ledger.py:53-59` [E3-installed], `cluster.py:67-70` [E1]). B depends on the four debate-resolved (`partial`) conflicts in these places: its launch adapters and class-launch grammar depend on C-03 (owner item O-2, the target environment), the decider on C-05, the round model on C-13, and the template and reviewer-count defaults on C-14 (Decision D-1). The architecture below leaves all four as declared hooks or owner-gated items, not as built behaviour.
3. **What the core owns and what stays per module follows the reflect contract.** Contract 1.8.0, `panel_lint` and the wrapper allow the core to own resolution, cards, clustering, citation checks, calibration coverage and manifests (all telemetry today), but not reflect's merge decision or contract writer without a bump (`research/07` Q8; C-11). Hence "verdict computed in the core, advisory-or-blocking policy and report format in each module" (C-19) and "the debate stays reflect's decider" (C-11).
4. **Decided conflicts are built as written; the four debate-resolved ones are staged behind hooks, gates or owner items.** Sixteen of the twenty conflicts are DECIDED-BY-EVIDENCE (C-01, C-02, C-04, C-06 to C-12, C-15 to C-20); their resolutions are adopted as written (within C-04, only the per-slot distinctness policy and no silent re-admission are decided; `replicas` is a design option). C-03, C-05, C-13 and C-14 were resolved by adversarial debate and every one ended `partial` (convergence blocked by unaddressed HIGH invariants); the architecture is not independent of them (Rationale 2). They are carried as open design risks in the table below, not as solved questions.
5. **Measured evidence is thin on quality everywhere, so the design buys measurement.** The only decider measured against ground truth is the single-reviewer veto, and that evidence is negative on precision (65 judge-blocking against 38 real; +2.75 to 3.25 wrong blockers when doubling two models) [E2] [DOC-SOURCED]; the grader, quorum, calibrator and debate have no accuracy data (C-05); 2 versus 3 reflect reviewers, Tier 2 versus Tier 1 recall (H-A) and rounds 2-3 under a multi-reviewer ledger were never measured (C-13, C-14). The architecture therefore keeps the veto live, records the grader and shadow signals beside it, and makes the shadow harness plus a portable E2-style benchmark part of the core (C-20) so the gates can be passed with data.
6. **Default panel size.** The reflect Tier 2 default stays at three reviewers with the hard cap of 3 (`_REFLECT_MAX_REVIEWERS = 3`, `panel.py:73`); the C-14 merge (Option B as merged, `reviews/adversarial/panel-size-cost/proposal-merged.md`) first records the escalation rows and the calibrated confidence at decision time (Step A: audit-only keys `matching_rows`, `calibrated_C_t1`, `S_domains_basis`, `tier_without_flag`, `sparse_flag_decisive`, `escalation_path` in `tier_decision.yaml`, and telemetry `reviewers_requested`, `reviewers_resolved`, `reviewers_source`); the parsed-sparse pre-filter (`SKILL.md:284,443`) stays unchanged unless those keys show it decisive (Conditional item CI-1, because the flag was decisive in 0 for certain and at most 1 of 39 runs); the cap stays 3; the count is decided after H-A on a symmetric pre-registered rule (Step C: three pinned arms, Tier 1, Tier 2 with two reviewers, Tier 2 with three) with Decision D-1 owner-gated (Step B). A skill-path default of two reviewers (9 of 38 recorded Tier 2 runs already ran two cards) is carried only as the owner-gated Decision D-1 of the C-14 merge; this report does not take it. Packaged template defaults (default 4 reviewers, `live7` 7, `review-panel.default.json:9-11`, `review-panel.live7.json:9-13`) are not changed by this recommendation (owner decision 6 of the C-14 merge: blocked on C-03).
7. **Cost trade-offs the owner must see.** B is the largest design surface; its registry layer is untested; the PR and rf-qa seams have open owner choices (finder versus S2b verifier, G-80; rubric versus router authority, G-78). The all-reviewer source injection of C-10 multiplies source tokens by the reviewer count and its cost was never measured (G-47); it interacts with D-1. The grader (C-05) is class-disjoint from the reviewers by design and is always `degraded` in the anthropic-only cell that C-03 selects (`research/05` section 12; C-06 Cons).
