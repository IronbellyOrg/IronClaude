## Steelman of Variant 1

V1's strongest argument is delivery reliability: a short inline rule still works when a reference is unread, while a registry and reverse scan catch drift and unregistered copies (sections 7.2 to 7.4 and 10.2).
Its skill location is genuinely better fitted to today's development sync: K3:112-125 copies skill packages, while the full sync target at K3:109-158 has no core-copy step.
Its extension contracts also turn future gates and judges into bounded additions instead of another rule rewrite (section 8).
Those are real maintainability advantages, not reasons to accept V1's uncertainty or confidence policies.

## Steelman of Variant 2

V2's strongest argument is that changing a definition cannot repair every source of excess review work: its RC2 consolidation issue is real, since K4:252 says "Preserve the highest severity rating when duplicates disagree."
Its proposed mechanical checks before verification address defects introduced by fixing, rather than hoping a classifier will excuse them (section 4.4 L2).
Its worked cases, blind non-blocker audit, seeded recall and explicit sample-size limits make the claimed benefit falsifiable (sections 5 and 8).
Its rejection of a high minimum as proof of joint confidence is also sound; repeated agreement is not proof of correctness.

## Strengths claimed

1. **Stable identity is a data-integrity requirement, not presentation polish.** V3 sections 4.2 and 4.3 separate items from observations and exclude moving line numbers and severity labels from identity.
   V2 section 6.2 instead uses "location plus a hash of the quote", which can change after an edit; V3 records ambiguous identity rather than guessing a new item.
   This is consistent with the existing need to deduplicate and retain originating lenses at K4:252, but extends it across reviews and resumes.
2. **A posting timeout needs reconciliation, not another create request.** V3 section 4.5 says "Never retry creation until absence or the existing issue has been established" and supplies a batch marker plus filing states.
   K8:19 says both issue modes post directly and K8:46 provides no preview or dry-run; local write-back alone therefore cannot close the remote-success/local-failure gap.
   V3 also binds the selected IDs and proposed body to consent, so an old answer cannot authorize a materially different batch.
3. **Missing evidence must not masquerade as negative evidence.** V3 section 5.3 explicitly requires SHOW_USER for missing decisive inputs and unresolved safety.
   Independently reading K1:35-46 shows that only S equal to U lifts a lower result; S equal to UNSET does not, and K1:29 permits FOLLOW_UP when wrong is Y and D is N.
   V3 therefore distinguishes the sketch's actual behavior from the policy a safe implementation must enforce.
4. **The judge boundary is an API contract.** V3 section 5.2 rejects wrong request IDs, duplicate or missing question IDs, malformed probabilities and synthetic certainty from reviewer booleans.
   Section 5.4 adds bounded attempts, a total budget, cache invalidation when evidence or decision basis changes, and privacy checks covering fallback judges too.
   These are proposed requirements, not verified service guarantees; vendor latency, availability and retention remain UNVERIFIED.
5. **Recording failure must remain visible.** V3 section 4.3 requires atomic revision-checked rewrites and preserves corrupt ledgers rather than resetting them to empty.
   Its worker return fields distinguish zero findings from incomplete capture, and its nested-ledger references fit the nested-contract separation at K2:24-25 and existing report/artifact split at K2:32-35.
   K2:41 also forbids outward worker defaults including paid external runs, supporting V3's separation of worker recording from coordinator-owned experiments.

## Weaknesses identified in the others

1. **V1's useful operational simplicity stops short of repeatable filing.** Its section 4.5 renders a description and writes the issue number back, but specifies no recovery after creation succeeds and the response is lost.
   K8:19 confirms the outward action happens directly; V3's prepared/posting/reconcile states address a failure V1's status field cannot distinguish.
2. **V2's strongest consumer test has an unsafe unknown branch.** Section 4.2 records an unestablished consumer as non-blocking Medium; section 8 later relies on a final sweep to catch downstream use.
   A later sweep is mitigation, not evidence at the present gate; K7:222 already distinguishes unreadable input from a valid zero-finding result.
   V3 correctly retains the existing protocol label or escalates instead of demoting because the necessary basis was unavailable.
3. **V1 treats a sketch shortcut as desired policy.** Section 5.2 calls the N/UNSET behavior "the more useful behaviour", but K1:21-24 confirms an unanswered input can participate in the negative result.
   K1:67-69 also excludes UNSET transitions from its monotonicity check; V1's test requirement "monotone in every input" needs narrowing, not repetition of the success banner.
4. **V1 and V2 do not close fallback privacy.** V1 decision D1 and V2 decision D3 propose the LLM judge when TypeSafe privacy is denied.
   Neither passage makes approval of that fallback's data processing an explicit precondition; V3 section 5.4 does, without assuming every fallback is external or prohibited.
5. **V1's ledger-unavailable path weakens the promised record guarantee.** Section 7.4 permits local report notes and no gate block when neither reference can be read.
   That fallback may preserve observations, but it must report incomplete central capture rather than imply successful record-and-offer completion; V3 sections 4.3 and 4.6 make this distinction.
6. **V2's consolidation remedy needs one consistent unresolved-dispute rule.** Section 4.4 L1 makes an undecidable dispute Medium, while section 8 GR5 says the blocking grade stands unless code disproves the named consumer.
   Prefer its evidence-based consolidation and mechanical checks, but resolve that internal conflict using V3's no-demotion-on-missing-evidence rule; do not import its accepted-blocker choice as an automatic pass.

## Concessions

1. I accept the diff's X-001 criticism of V3: its named project development surface is proposed, not presently generated; K3:109-158 confirms the missing core-sync step.
   V3 does acknowledge that gap, but the final spec must not present the path as current capability; prefer V1's skill package unless a core-delivery change is explicitly justified.
2. I concede S-003 and S-004 to V2: V3 needs its cause/remedy model and worked cases, especially cases where an upstream consumer is not yet established.
3. I concede X-002 to V1: concise inline digest plus pointer is more resilient than V3's preference for references alone; version checks do not make an unread rule understood.
4. I concede X-004 on interruption cost: keep V3's consent snapshot, but aggregate offers once per top-level run as V1 recommends, rather than ask separately for every completed loop.
5. I accept A-009: K1:5 lists no UNDETERMINED outcome and K1:41-43 routes all-unjudged wrong inputs to SHOW_USER; V3 must define a pre-table typed failure that advances the judge chain.
6. I concede C-020 and X-013 completeness: V3's extra transitive pin does not make its test inventory exhaustive; the final inventory must combine both additional modules identified in the diff and recheck them before edits.
7. V3's four-table design costs more than a display list, but filing states are justified only when filing is enabled; empty tables must not force an external service, extra prompts or duplicate report copies.
8. V3 does not prove ten paid questions outperform six; adopt V2's six decisive questions as the minimum and keep explanatory questions optional until measured.
9. V3's numerical bounds are not calibrated probabilities of correctness; keep null or an explicit uncalibrated label until held-out evidence exists, as V2 emphasizes.
