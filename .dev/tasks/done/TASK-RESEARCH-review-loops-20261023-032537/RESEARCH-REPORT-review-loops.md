## 1. Problem Statement

- **The question:** Why do plans generated through `/sc:workflow` and tasks executed through `/sc:implement` receive further unsuccessful review results after corrections? For each observed sequence, what failed, what changed, what did the next reviewer find, and was that finding introduced by the correction, already present, left unresolved, based on changed criteria, unsupported, or unknowable from retained evidence?
- **Why it matters:** Calling every later finding a regression hides successful repairs and old defects. Calling every passing check or accepted completion full correctness hides unmet requirements, evidence gaps and changes in review policy.
- **Trigger:** The user reports fixes leading to more failures and wants causal analysis, not another speculative remediation loop.
- **Method and deliverable:** The investigation began with a frozen latest-30-session population and selected six primary repeated-review incidents for dedicated analysis. It preserves a local evidence deck and separates the observed mechanisms before any subsequent debate or ranking of five candidate causes. Sections 1-5 describe that evidence. The provisional, non-consensus comparative ranking of the five candidates is in Section 7.
- **Requested ranking criterion and the criterion delivered:** The build request asks for the five candidates to be "ranked most to least likely" and says: "Use the result to rank the five by likelihood given this sample, not by artifact polish ... No fabricated probability percentages" (`BUILD-REQUEST.md:12,34`). The debate's diff analysis recorded that the five cards "await comparative likelihood ranking" but that "none supplies a common comparison rule that resolves breadth versus event-specific causal certainty", and that "existence, explanatory reach and expected recurrence are different questions". It therefore promoted A-001 as an open shared assumption: each advocate had to accept, reject or qualify it and give a comparison rule, and its impact note says that strong narrow evidence "is not automatically less likely than broader evidence" (`reviews/adversarial/adversarial/diff-analysis.md:64,69`). The rule itself came from the advocates: in Rounds 1 and 2 they ranked explanatory reach across contexts first and direct evidence for a particular transition second, and in Round 2 the V2-V5 advocates said the result was not population or measured likelihood (`reviews/adversarial/adversarial/debate-transcript.md:47,65,143,182,196,277,316,356,397,438`; Section 6.0). Section 7 ranks explanatory support under A-001, not likelihood. No user authorization for that substitution was on record when the ranking was made; on 2026-10-24 the user accepted the explanatory-support order as the delivered answer, with no re-ranking (task log entry `[2026-10-24T06:32Z]`).
