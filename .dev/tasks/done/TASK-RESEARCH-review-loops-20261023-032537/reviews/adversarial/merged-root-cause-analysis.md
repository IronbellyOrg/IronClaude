# Why Repair and Review Kept Reopening

<!-- Source: Variants 1-5, Hypothesis and Summary; Round 2 shared explanatory target; Change #1. -->
Checks that proved less than the requirement provide the broadest supported explanation in this selected sample. They are not the whole explanation: some repairs introduced connected defects, review contracts changed, known blockers remained outside permitted correction or available verification, and proof handoffs failed independently of delivery.

All five causes remain distinct and can coexist. A sequence can begin with weak verification, continue through a successful partial repair, and fail again because a different original defect or an unchanged blocker remains.

## Scope and Comparison Rule

<!-- Source: Variants 1-5, Scope and Evidence Strength/Limits; Round 2 comparison rule; Changes #1 and #8. -->
The target is why an apparent repair or pass was followed by another failed review or required review entry in six selected historical sessions. This includes both substantive rejection and policy-driven reopening; it is not limited to defects newly created by repairs.

The comparison gives explanatory reach across relevant contexts priority, then direct evidence of the particular transition. Strong event-specific evidence overrides the overall ordering. Related packages, multiple findings and repeated reviewer messages are not independent causal observations.

The sample was selected by recent file modification and is clustered. It establishes concrete mechanisms, not population prevalence, causal probabilities, a general model-performance comparison or a version effect.

The selected writing base is brittle evidence and provenance handoffs (Variant 4). That document won a writing-selection process; its selection does not make that cause most likely. Agreement on ten writing-synthesis points likewise does not establish agreement on causal order.

## Five Candidate Accounts

### Checks Prove Less Than the Requirement

<!-- Source: Variant 1, Exact Mechanism, Supporting Incidents, Counterevidence, Scope and Discriminating Test; Changes #3-4 and #6-8. -->
**Mechanism.** A behavioral requirement is replaced by a narrower predicate: parsing, labels, counts, markers or validation-step presence. Early checks genuinely pass without proving semantics, execution order, cross-component use or exceptional paths. A later review finds an old counterexample rather than damage caused by the immediately preceding fix.

**Supporting evidence.** The [oracle case][oracle] records wrong known/unknown-author and default-path security meanings despite passing fixture labels/counts and initial tests. Historical parent transcript record 291 and the preserved first/second review artifacts anchor this initial escape.

The [monitor case][monitor] combines three jobs and many behaviors in a 640-line workflow. Current-task acceptance is structural while lasting behavior tests are deferred to the later test task (Task 14). Later range, summary and size findings include defects already present in the original workflow; historical records 585, 804, 906 and 1021 anchor the sequence.

The [Orca planning case][planning] credits an up-to-date state not named by the original tasks and places validation after the fetch that consumes its input. The first coverage matrix records the former at historical artifact lines 1749-1757; the second merged findings record the latter at lines 39-47.

The [continuous-integration planning case][ci] checks pull-request creation arguments while its search stub ignores effective filters. Stronger phrase blacklists still accept contradictory advisory wording. Presence or a blacklist is therefore not equivalent to the required behavior.

**Opposition and alternatives.** Three permitted oracle repairs worked; its repeated fourth finding concerns frozen correction authority, not another weak-check escape. Smaller monitor tasks (Tasks 10/11) converged after one repair. Proven inventory/trap introductions require the connected-effects account.

**Conditional fit and limits.** This leads when an old bad state satisfies the original checks. Size and deferred tests are observational amplifiers, not isolated causes; why particular reviewers missed counterexamples remains unknown.

**Discriminating comparison.** On the preserved first-passing version, compare the actual check with a criterion-derived minimal counterexample while fixing original criteria and policy. Support increases if the weak check accepts wrong behavior that the behavioral check rejects; it weakens if the checks actually used already reject it. This replay was not executed. See the [weak-check source card][v1].

### Review Contracts Change During the Loop

<!-- Source: Variant 3, Exact Mechanism, Supporting Incidents, Counterevidence, Scope and Discriminating Test; Changes #3-4 and #6-8. -->
**Mechanism.** Written requirements, early interpretation and later loaded review rules differ. Keep four subtypes separate: unresolved source conflicts, interpretation changes, added hardening and policy migration. They can change a verdict or require another review without creating a new delivery defect.

**Supporting evidence.** In [credential finals][credentials], the lint task (Task 6) accepts no-new-findings although the literal condition prohibits findings on changed lines. Final review reapplies the original clause; owner acceptance comes later. Parent records 1215, 1230, 1935 and 1938, with nested Task 6 reviewer record 61, preserve this relaxation/restoration.

The same run captures two materially different final-review rubrics. The older text treats stale evidence as issues; the later text says a moved line alone is not issues and changes acceptance handling. The [captured historical rule evidence][rules] preserves the loaded bytes, not a reconstruction from today's policy.

In the [monitor case][monitor], migration requires a fresh operator review after an earlier compliant verdict. Historical records 42, 58, 849, 874, 893 and 902 preserve that entry change. The new review finds genuine preexisting omissions. No adviser/revert ladder executed for this task.

Some monitor findings add broader risk checking, including uncapped fields absent from the plan or case-insensitive matching not clearly required. Later authorization does not make these retroactively original criteria. [Planning source conflicts][planning] and the [continuous-integration boundary-file dispute][ci] separately demonstrate disagreement over source or repair scope.

**Opposition and alternatives.** Oracle review repeats the unchanged default-path requirement. Manual-page review twice requests the same actual rendering proof. These stable-contract repeats are counterexamples to contract change as their explanation. Broader checking is not automatically invalid.

**Conditional fit and limits.** This leads for demonstrated review re-entry or rule-driven reversal, not creation of old defects. The actor changing the installed rubric is unknown. Final credential convergence follows both proof correction and policy change; neither effect is isolated.

**Discriminating comparison.** Hold an artifact state fixed and adjudicate separately under preserved original criteria and the actually loaded rules, retaining authorized exceptions. Classify original violation, authorized acceptance, added hardening, unresolved contradiction or rule-version effect. Unchanged substantive failure under identical conditions weakens this account. No replay was executed. See the [contract-change source card][v3].

### Local Repairs Miss Connected Effects

<!-- Source: Variant 2, Exact Mechanism, Supporting Incidents, Counterevidence, Scope and Discriminating Test; Changes #3-4 and #6-8. -->
**Mechanism.** A local remedy crosses an unchecked execution-order, dependency, data-source or output-stream boundary. Two outcomes must remain separate: a demonstrably introduced defect and an untouched sibling failure. Evidence of the second alone does not establish the first.

**Supporting evidence.** In [Orca planning][planning], an added inventory comparison becomes required in the inventory-test task (Task 13), before the next task registers the workflow (Task 14). Intermediate acceptance is then unsatisfiable. Successful historical writes are recorded at parent physical records 544-545 and 841; the second merged findings identify the conflict at historical artifact lines 120-128.

The same planning amendments reuse a four-asset eligibility predicate at release detection, crossing the distinction between release existence and pin-writer eligibility. A digest-timing repair also selects deployed-package provenance where the companion field requires recipe provenance. These are separate connected mistakes within one planning cluster, not independent sessions.

In the [monitor case][monitor], a successful repair enables inherited error traps that print diagnostics to standard output (stdout). Failed command substitution captures those annotations as data and can emit them again outside. Parent records 759-762 preserve the introduction and record 1240 preserves the reproduced failure. This is actual implementation-regression evidence.

In [continuous-integration planning][ci], repointed dependencies lose required ancestors. The intended executor's sequential order prevents demonstrated runtime reordering, but does not make the dependency graph conform to the requirement.

**Opposition and alternatives.** Fourteen original planning amendments were accepted and two were partial. Twenty later numbered items are not twenty repair regressions. Most later monitor defects preexist; removing an argument-size failure exposes later existing output limits rather than creating them. Oracle repairs and proof-only credential changes also supply successful or differently explained transitions.

**Conditional fit and limits.** This leads for a failure absent immediately before and present after a confirmed successful amendment under the original contract. Historical plan nonconformance is not automatically observed execution disorder. The monitor trap was accepted with known defects, not shown repaired.

**Discriminating comparison.** Replay confirmed successful edits against their immediate preserved predecessor, holding criteria/policy fixed and tracing the affected producer, consumer, sibling path or dependency. Require an absent-before/present-after counterexample. If already present, classify retained or previously missed. No replay was executed. See the [connected-effects source card][v2].

### Late Feasibility and Repair-Authority Checks

<!-- Source: Variant 5, Exact Mechanism, Supporting Incidents, Counterevidence, Scope and Falsification Test; Changes #3-6 and #8. -->
**Mechanism.** Review is reached before checking either permission to correct a blocking input or capability to produce required evidence. These are distinct barriers. A resubmission leaves the already-identified failed property unchanged, so another valid rejection follows.

**Supporting evidence.** In the [oracle case][oracle], first-repair authority explicitly excludes the known `default_path=false` fixture. The second review confirms three successful repairs and repeats only that original defect. Later authorization enables a one-field correction and PASS. Parent records 291, 383, 398, 450, 521, 573 and 668 anchor the sequence.

In the [documentation case][documentation], the available manual-page command prints a notice rather than rendering. Its nominal zero exit status does not establish capability. A static-only retry changes no text and leaves the same real-render clause unverified. Parent records 413-414, 430, 433-434, 443, 450, 506 and 527 preserve the attempts and scoped acceptance.

**Opposition and alternatives.** Neither original requirement was intrinsically impossible. Correct fixture data could have been authored before freezing, and a functioning renderer could satisfy the document clause elsewhere. No malformed manual page was demonstrated.

Freezing is a legitimate anti-overfitting control. Re-review usefully confirmed repaired portions while retaining the blocker; it was not inherently pointless. Weak semantic checks and an inadequate availability guard help explain earlier admission, but do not remove the later authority/capability barrier.

**Conditional fit and limits.** This leads for documented unchanged blockers, not every repeated finding. Its direct reach is two incidents. Authorized fixture correction/PASS and accepted unverified rendering are different endpoints.

**Discriminating comparison.** Identify the failed property, actual evidence-producing capability and permitted correction before comparing attempts. Determine whether the next attempt changes the blocker or obtains the required scoped decision. An unsupported repeat after demonstrated permitted resolution would weaken the account. No comparison, installation, freeze bypass or task execution occurred. See the [retained-blocker source card][v5].

A missing checkpoint classified as “head-moved” does not itself establish actual repository movement.

**Supporting evidence.** In the initial [credential package][credentials], genuine temporary lint proof becomes an unrelated README separator citation. **Opposition and alternatives.** Initial proof rejection was valid under its loaded evidence contract. The follow-up also contains a genuine literal lint failure, so proof weakness does not explain its whole rejection. Final convergence combines pointer correction and changed rubric text.

Unchanged delivery digests are useful controls, but exclude evidence artifacts and policy. They do not prove unchanged proof, unchanged rubric or substantive compliance. A corrected attribution is not a delivery repair; a checkpoint halt is not the rendering-failure cause.

**Conditional fit and limits.** This leads for isolated proof rejection or false attribution. Support is direct but concentrated in credential/documentation work; related packages are not independent breadth. Why the first audit missed the blank pointer remains unknown.

## What a Later Pass Actually Establishes

<!-- Source: Variants 2-5, Counterevidence, Scope, Gaps and Summary; Change #5. -->
- **Corrected compliance:** The fixture case later records authorized correction and PASS.
- **Corrected proof:** The initial credential interval records required proof/pointer repair without delivery changes.
- **Confounded convergence:** The later credential pass follows proof fixes and changed policy; their separate contributions are unknown.
- **Authorized acceptance:** The monitor trap continuation accepts known defects rather than demonstrating repair.
- **Accepted inability to verify:** Manual-page rendering remains unestablished despite scoped acceptance.
- **Guard halt:** Missing start-checkpoint evidence establishes a halt classification, not actual Git HEAD movement.

These endpoints are not interchangeable. Neither an accepted gap nor a compliant earlier check establishes every later requirement, and unchanged delivery does not establish unchanged review inputs.

## How to Distinguish These Causes

<!-- Source: Variants 1-3, Discriminating Test; Variants 4-5, Falsification Test; independent sufficiency challenge; Change #6. -->
The required evidence path is: original criterion, actual check, permitted successful repair, neighboring invariants, durable meaningful proof, historical fixed rubric, then verified endpoint. All comparisons below remain proposed and unexecuted.

1. Preserve immediate historical states and original criteria; do not substitute mutable present-day files.
2. Test whether an old counterexample passes the checks actually used, separately from a criterion-derived behavioral check.
3. Attribute introduction only to a confirmed successful change with an absent-before/present-after counterexample and a traced connected effect.
4. Hold artifact state fixed when comparing original and actually changed contracts; retain exceptions and added scope explicitly.
5. Hold delivery and rubric fixed when isolating proof quality, and reconstruct the truthful baseline before attributing changes.
6. For a retained blocker, separately establish correction authority and actual evidence-producing capability; identify what the next attempt changes.
7. Record whether the endpoint is correction, proof repair, an authorized exception, inability to verify or a guard halt.

No single check is shown sufficient. Stronger fixture checking does not itself grant correction authority; improved static evidence does not establish rendering; a local remedy can violate a neighboring contract. Smaller tasks alone are not a demonstrated universal cure. These comparisons do not authorize waiving valid criteria or replacing independent final review.
