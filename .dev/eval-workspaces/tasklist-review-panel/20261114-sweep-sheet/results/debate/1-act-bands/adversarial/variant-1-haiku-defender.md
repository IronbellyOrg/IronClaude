## 1 Verdict

**PASS** — registering these explicitly unmeasured planning bands does not itself distort adoption decisions, because the stated gates do not use them (F1, F6).

## 2 Position summary

The strongest defense is limited but substantive: these are frozen expectations, not calibrated confidence intervals or acceptance thresholds. The design explicitly labels them planning values and replaces planning probabilities with measured rates; adoption has separate effect, consistency, precision and non-regression criteria (F1, F6; MR:1075–1102). A weak forecast is not automatically an unfair experiment. PASS does not mean the bands have empirical support.

## 3 Answers to the attacks

**(a) Derived from unmeasured estimates.** Correct. Their origin is disclosed, not concealed (F1–F2). Pre-registration can record an investigator's expectations before observing outcomes; requiring measured hold-out estimates first would defeat that purpose. The nine issue-level ranges supply traceable reasoning, not statistical calibration. They must not be described as established performance.

**(b) Too wide or too narrow.** Neither criticism establishes a blocking defect for descriptive expectations. Wide ranges reduce informativeness; narrow ranges make forecasts fragile. F9 decisively defeats an *all observed rates must fall inside* test: C3 permits no outcome at five trials. But that is not a stated gate. These ranges should not be mistaken for sampling intervals.

**(c) Outside its band.** The documents specify no special penalty. The stated outcome is that measured class rates replace planning probabilities (MR:1102; F7). Report the departure, denominator and uncertainty; do not retune the frozen range or invent a new adoption criterion. This is an interpretation of their existing non-gating role, not evidence that a formal discrepancy test exists.

**(d) Unfair arm invalidation.** Not under the listed rules. G0 and D1–D7 have explicit criteria independent of these bands (F6; MR:1081–1091). Rejecting an arm merely for exceeding an upper bound would punish improvement using an unstated criterion. A later analyst could misuse the bands, but that misuse is not prescribed by registration.

**(e) Few hold-out items.** REQ-41 expressly requires per-class registration (F7). Sparse classes limit inference, not the ability to record expectations. Report zero-denominator classes as unestimable, not failures. Correlated repeats do not create independent evidence, and low power is acknowledged (F8). No claim of class-wide validation follows from falling inside a range.

**(f) Pooled or measured-Z0 band instead.** Neither is a necessary replacement. Pooling can conceal class-specific weaknesses and depends on class weights; a simple average implicitly chooses equal weights. A Z0-relative band answers comparative improvement, whereas these numbers record absolute planning expectations. Existing adoption gates already compare with Z0. Replacing all class bands with one pooled band would also abandon REQ-41's explicit form (F6–F7).

## 4 Exact registration text I would commit

“Register C1 [0.10,0.40], C2 [0.20,0.45], C3 [0.03,0.15], C4 [0.50,0.75], C5 [0.35,0.60], C6 [0.05,0.30], C7 [0.15,0.35], C8 [0.40,0.65] as unvalidated planning expectations, not adoption thresholds or confidence intervals. Preserve their original definitions and provenance. Report measured stage rates separately; no outside-band result changes gate outcomes. The historical estimates and operational act stage are not directly comparable.”

## 5 Concessions

F3–F4 expose a real definition mismatch: historical act factors out selection and includes survival; operational act includes selection misses and excludes survival. I cannot defend a direct numerical calibration claim. My PASS rests on the documented planning-only, non-gating interpretation, not on pretending the quantities agree. The registration paragraph makes that limitation explicit without changing endpoints.

## 6 What evidence would make me switch to FAIL

A requirement to test measured PROBLEM rates directly against these ranges; an outside-band rejection rule; or reporting that treats departures as reviewer failures despite selection/survival differences. Any would turn the conceded mismatch and sparse-sample arithmetic into a blocking, misleading comparison.
