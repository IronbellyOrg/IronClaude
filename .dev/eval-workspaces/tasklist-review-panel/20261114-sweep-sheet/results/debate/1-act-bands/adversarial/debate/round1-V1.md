**PASS, narrowly:** the endpoints may be registered as disclosed planning expectations; neither numerical calibration nor an outside-band acceptance test is established (F1, F6–F7). PASS does not certify the definitions match.

## Steelman, then critique

**V2 steelman.** Its strongest case is semantic, not statistical: registering a tolerance under the same name as a differently defined measurement invites selection failures to be blamed on reviewers, contrary to the stage split's stated purpose (F3–F5). Owner confirmation is a real procedural gate, so calling the registration wholly inert understates its significance (F6). This genuinely warrants explicit warnings.

**V2 critique.** Its worked multiplication cannot establish the claimed numerical failures: historical act already includes survival, whereas operational act excludes it (F3–F4); multiplying historical act by selection estimates a survival-inclusive quantity, not operational act. Its proposed consequence also adds a new dependency: F7 supplies no rule making REQ-45 wording conditional on interval overlap. That is a possible improvement, not proof the default violates that requirement. Finally, F9 defeats a point-in-band test, not every descriptive use of the bands.

**V3 steelman.** Its strongest contribution is specifying scope, denominator and permitted consequences before outcomes arrive. Separate materials and a distinct-item floor confront real heterogeneity and repeated-item dependence (F2, F8). Its report-only alternative protects adoption gates while making reporting more reproducible.

**V3 critique.** Excluding survival does not preserve the historical quantity either (F3). The proposed rule therefore changes the target rather than merely clarifying it. Moreover, a distinct-item minimum does not make correlated item-runs independent: the Wilson rule's advertised false-alarm protection remains unestablished for this design (F8–F9). A more elaborate registration can itself mislead if that limitation is obscured. These alternatives do not demonstrate that registering disclosed expectations alone makes the experiment unfair or unmeasurable.
