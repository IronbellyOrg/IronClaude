## Socratic Dialogue Record (enterprise / deep tier — 14 questions)

### Frame batch (incident commander to system)

**Q1. Time-boxed scope: are we post-morteming a single incident, a cluster, or the whole quarter?**
A: A cluster. Three Sev-2 incidents in Q1 (Jan 18, Feb 9, Mar 27), all "payment webhooks not delivered to merchants within SLO". Different surface symptoms each time, same systemic blast pattern (queue backlog → retry storm → DLQ overflow → merchant-side reconciliation gaps).

**Q2. Customer impact, quantified?**
A: 412 unique merchants affected across the three incidents, $1.4M in delayed settlements, 2 customer-success escalations to VP level, 1 contract renewal at risk (Tier-1 enterprise merchant cited webhook reliability as a renewal blocker in their QBR). Compliance angle: PCI-DSS 10.2 requires audit trail of all transaction notifications — DLQ overflow may have lost evidence.

**Q3. Has any of this been triaged or just operational firefighting?**
A: Each incident has a P0 ticket, a 24-hour PIR (post-incident review), and a remediation backlog item. None of the three remediation items have been completed — they keep getting deprioritized because each "felt different" at the time. This brainstorm exists because Engineering Director called the pattern.

**Q4. Who owns the webhook delivery system today?**
A: Payments Platform team (8 engineers, 1 EM). Webhook dispatcher is a Go service (`payments-webhook-dispatcher`), backing store is PostgreSQL + Redis Streams, downstream is HTTP POST to merchant-supplied URLs with exponential backoff retry up to 24h.

### Investigate batch (commander to evidence)

**Q5. What did the three PIRs actually conclude as root cause?**
A: PIR-1 (Jan 18): "Redis Streams consumer lag during traffic spike from one large merchant." PIR-2 (Feb 9): "Database connection pool exhaustion after a slow query was introduced in a release." PIR-3 (Mar 27): "DLQ processor crash-looped after a malformed payload from a merchant returning an oversized response body that exceeded our parser limit." Each PIR treated the proximate cause as the root cause.

**Q6. Is there a common upstream signal across all three?**
A: Yes — in retrospect. The Webhook-Delivery-Latency P99 metric drifted upward for ~36h before each incident. Nobody had it on a dashboard or an alert. The metric exists; nobody owned it.

**Q7. What does the retry / DLQ architecture look like?**
A: Three tiers. (1) In-process retry with exponential backoff (5 attempts over ~15 min). (2) Redis-Streams-based delayed retry (8 more attempts over ~24h). (3) Dead-Letter Queue (PostgreSQL table) for manual replay. DLQ overflow happens because the table has no TTL and no compaction — and the Mar 27 incident pushed it past the 2M-row mark where its index scans got slow enough to back up the producers.

**Q8. Are merchants doing anything contributing to the failure mode?**
A: Yes, two pathological patterns: (a) some merchants return 200 OK then process asynchronously and silently drop on their side — we treat as delivered, they have a gap; (b) some merchants have webhook endpoints behind authentication that periodically rotates without notifying us, so our delivery attempts 401 for hours before they notice. Neither is malicious; both are systemic.

**Q9. What's the security/compliance surface?**
A: PCI-DSS scope. Webhook bodies do NOT contain PAN, but they contain transaction IDs, merchant IDs, and timing data. Loss of delivery evidence is a compliance event under §10.2. Also: webhook delivery infrastructure is a credential-handling surface — merchants' webhook secrets are HMAC-signing keys. Any leakage in logs is a HIGH-severity event.

### Validate batch (commander to organization)

**Q10. What are the explicit non-negotiables from leadership for the remediation?**
A: (i) No degradation to throughput SLO (currently 8K webhooks/sec sustained, 25K peak). (ii) Zero data loss for webhooks already accepted into the system. (iii) PCI evidence chain must be preserved end-to-end (delivery attempt → final state → audit log retention 18 months). (iv) Rollout must be contract-safe: existing merchant integrations cannot break.

**Q11. What's the deploy/test surface look like?**
A: Production-shadow environment exists (synthetic merchants, real traffic shape via tcpreplay). Staging is unreliable for load — production-shadow is the only credible perf gate. Chaos tooling exists (Litmus) but is not currently used against this system.

**Q12. Who owns the runbook? Is on-call confident with the system?**
A: Runbook is 3 years old, last updated after a Sev-1 in 2023. Current on-call rotation: 4 engineers, only 2 have actually triaged a webhook DLQ incident. The other 2 escalate to the EM. This is itself a finding.

**Q13. Is there an SLO contract with merchants?**
A: Soft. Public docs say "webhooks delivered within 60 seconds 99% of the time"; no penalty structure, no per-merchant credits. Enterprise contracts often add bespoke language. The Tier-1 merchant at renewal risk has a 99.9% / 30s clause that we've been silently breaching for two quarters.

**Q14. What's the rollback / contain-the-blast-radius plan if remediation itself goes wrong?**
A: Feature flag per remediation component. Per-merchant kill switch already exists. Shadow-mode (new path runs, results compared to old path, neither is canonical) supported in dispatcher. Per-region rollout (US-East canary → US-West → EU → APAC, 1 week between regions). All four levers available; we just have to plan to use them.

## Problem Statement

Three Sev-2 webhook-delivery incidents in Q1 share a common pattern (latency drift → backlog → retry storm → DLQ overflow → merchant-side reconciliation gaps) but were each PIR'd as discrete proximate causes (Redis lag, DB pool exhaustion, DLQ crash-loop). 412 merchants affected, $1.4M in delayed settlements, 1 enterprise renewal at risk, and a PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence-chain concern. The remediation backlog from each PIR remains uncompleted because each incident "felt different." The brainstorm scope is to produce an enterprise-grade remediation requirements set spanning architecture, observability, security/compliance, on-call readiness, and the SLO contract — explicitly NOT a patch for the most recent incident.

## Known Context

- System: `payments-webhook-dispatcher` (Go service), PostgreSQL + Redis Streams, HTTP POST to merchant URLs.
- Throughput: 8K/sec sustained, 25K peak. Current SLO posture: public 60s/99%, enterprise contracts often 30s/99.9%.
- Retry: in-process (5 attempts / 15min) → Redis Streams delayed retry (8 attempts / 24h) → PostgreSQL DLQ (no TTL, no compaction — 2M-row threshold has been observed to cause producer back-pressure).
- Common upstream signal across all 3 incidents: Webhook-Delivery-Latency P99 drifted upward for ~36h pre-incident; unalerted, unowned.
- Compliance: PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence-chain (transaction-ID + delivery attempt outcome, 18-month retention). HMAC signing keys per merchant are HIGH-severity credentials.
- Merchant-side pathologies: (a) 200-OK-then-drop, (b) auth-rotation-without-notification.
- Runbook is 3 years stale. 2 of 4 on-call engineers have never solo-triaged this surface.
- Tier-1 enterprise renewal at risk; webhook reliability cited as a renewal blocker.

## Constraints

- Zero data loss for accepted webhooks (firm).
- No throughput SLO degradation during/after remediation rollout.
- PCI evidence chain preserved end-to-end through any architectural change.
- Per-merchant kill switch and per-region rollout must remain available throughout rollout.
- Production-shadow is the only credible perf gate (staging is unreliable for load).
- 24-hour total retry window must remain a configurable upper bound (downward-only without merchant notice; upward requires DPA review).
- Existing merchant integrations cannot break — wire-format compatible HMAC, headers, body shape.

## Success Criteria

- All three Q1 incident patterns produce no Sev-2 in next two quarters (lagging proxy: zero Sev-2-or-worse webhook incidents for Q2 and Q3).
- Tier-1 enterprise merchant renewal closes; webhook reliability is no longer a cited concern in their QBR.
- DLQ growth bounded: < 50K rows steady-state, < 500K under worst-quarter load, with compaction/TTL preventing index-scan back-pressure.
- Latency-drift signal owned: P99 webhook-delivery-latency on the on-call's primary dashboard, alerted at +25% over 7-day rolling baseline.
- PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence chain audited and certified by internal compliance team prior to GA.
- Runbook refreshed; all 4 on-call engineers have run a tabletop incident drill and can solo-triage.
- Per-merchant SLO observability: every merchant can see their own delivery percentile in a self-serve dashboard.

## Open Questions

- Sliding-window vs. per-tenant queue isolation: do we shard the dispatcher per-merchant-tier, or run a single shared pipeline with QoS classes?
- DLQ semantics: time-bounded (drop after 7d) or unbounded with merchant-pull replay API?
- Merchant-side failure detection: do we expose a "your endpoint has been failing for N hours" auto-notification, or wait for them to ask?
- SLO contract rewrite: do we attach explicit penalties (service credits) to the 99.9%/30s clause for enterprise, or keep it informational?
- Authentication-rotation contract: do we require merchants to register a key-rotation webhook, or detect and notify on 401-streak heuristically?
- Cross-region failover: active-active vs. active-passive for the dispatcher itself — current posture is single-region with read-replica DR.
- HMAC-key rotation: do we initiate a quarterly forced rotation for all merchants, or wait for the next compliance audit cycle?
- Observability ownership: a metric without an owner is the precondition of all three Q1 incidents — what's the structural fix (RACI doc, dashboard ownership, alert routing change)?
