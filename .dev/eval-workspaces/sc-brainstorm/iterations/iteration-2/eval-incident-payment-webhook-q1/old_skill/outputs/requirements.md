## Phase 0 — Incident Context

**Domain detection**: Topic matches incident keywords ("incident", "failures") and includes a time-window scope ("Q1"). Treated as multi-incident post-mortem.

### Source incidents

- Jan 18 PIR — Redis Streams consumer lag during traffic spike from large merchant.
- Feb 9 PIR — Database connection pool exhaustion after a slow query.
- Mar 27 PIR — DLQ processor crash-loop on malformed payload from merchant.

Each PIR concluded a different proximate cause and treated it as the root cause. None of the remediation backlog items has been completed.

### System sketch

Payments Platform team (8 eng, 1 EM). Service: `payments-webhook-dispatcher` (Go). State: PostgreSQL + Redis Streams. Dispatch: HTTP POST to merchant URLs with exponential backoff up to 24h. Throughput: ~8K/s sustained, ~25K/s peak.

### Impact

412 merchants affected, $1.4M delayed settlements, 1 Tier-1 enterprise renewal cited as at risk. PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence retention concerns mentioned in audit prep.

## Phase 1 — Socratic Exploration

Eight questions probing the incident:

**Q1. What's the common pattern across the three incidents?**
A: Each starts with rising latency, progresses to retry backlog, ends with DLQ overflow and merchant-side reconciliation gaps. PIRs treated each as distinct because the proximate trigger differed.

**Q2. Is there a common leading indicator?**
A: Webhook-Delivery-Latency P99 metric drifted upward ~36h before each incident. The metric exists; it was not on a dashboard or alert.

**Q3. What's the retry architecture?**
A: 3 tiers — in-process (5 attempts/15min) → Redis Streams delayed retry (8 attempts/24h) → PostgreSQL DLQ (unbounded, no TTL/compaction). DLQ hit ~2M rows during Mar 27, causing producer back-pressure.

**Q4. Are merchants contributing to the failure mode?**
A: Yes. Two pathologies: (a) merchants that 200-OK then drop async on their side, (b) merchants with rotating auth that don't notify us before rotation.

**Q5. What's the compliance surface?**
A: PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence chain. Webhook bodies don't contain PAN but contain transaction IDs and timing data. HMAC signing keys are high-severity credentials.

**Q6. What's the SLO posture?**
A: Public 60s/99%; enterprise contracts often 30s/99.9%. We have been silently breaching the enterprise clauses for two quarters.

**Q7. Is the runbook current?**
A: 3 years old. Last updated post-2023 Sev-1. Only 2 of 4 on-call engineers have actually triaged this surface.

**Q8. What's the rollback / safe-rollout surface?**
A: Per-merchant kill switch exists. Feature flags supported. Per-region rollout possible. Shadow-mode supported.

## Phase 2 — Multi-Persona Analysis

Three personas coordinated in-context. ~300 words each.

### Analyzer

Root cause is debatable. The three PIRs each named a different proximate cause (Redis lag, DB pool, DLQ crash-loop). All true. But the cross-incident pattern (latency drift → backlog → cascade → DLQ overflow) suggests a common upstream weakness: the latency metric is unowned, no alert, no dashboard. Fix the unowned-metric problem and you get 36h of lead time on whatever the next proximate cause turns out to be. Alternative framing: the 3-tier retry pipeline has no back-pressure between tiers, so a small disturbance amplifies into a Sev-2. Both true; both worth addressing.

Risk inventory: (a) cascading failure between retry tiers (lack of back-pressure), (b) unbounded DLQ growth (back-pressures producers when index scans slow), (c) merchant-side reconciliation gaps not measured (200-OK-then-drop), (d) auth-rotation pathologies that we can detect heuristically.

### Security

PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence chain is the highest-stake finding here. Two facts together are concerning: (i) DLQ has no TTL and no compaction, so retention is implicit and unaudited; (ii) merchant-side 200-OK-then-drop means our "delivered" count is over-counted by an unknown margin. Either alone is a finding. Together they say our evidence chain has gaps we don't measure. HMAC signing keys are also a concern: rotation cadence unclear, log redaction posture unverified. Recommend: bounded DLQ with audit-logged eviction, merchant-ack callback for high-tier merchants, quarterly HMAC rotation, log-redaction scan before any rotation announcement (rotate-then-discover is worse than discover-then-rotate).

### DevOps

Operational maturity is the wrapper that determines whether any technical fix lands. Runbook is 3 years stale; tabletop drills don't happen; SLO contract vs. delivered numbers don't match. Recommend: runbook rewrite, quarterly tabletops, SLO contract alignment (publish what we measure, measure what we promise), per-region rollout playbook for all remediations, owner field on every dashboard panel. The "metric exists, nobody owns it" pattern from Analyzer's root-cause framing is operationalized as a RACI / owner-field requirement.

## Phase 4 — Specification

### Functional Requirements

1. **FR-1**: System SHALL alert on P99 webhook-delivery-latency drift (+25% over 7-day baseline).
2. **FR-2**: System SHALL implement per-tier bulkhead queue isolation (Tier-1 / Tier-2 / Tier-3).
3. **FR-3**: System SHALL implement bounded DLQ with audit-logged eviction; cryptographic chain-of-custody for Tier-1.
4. **FR-4**: System SHALL implement cross-tier back-pressure between retry tiers.
5. **FR-5**: System SHALL support merchant-ack callback for Tier-1 merchants (Tier-2 opt-in).
6. **FR-6**: System SHALL rotate HMAC signing keys quarterly with a 14-day overlap window.
7. **FR-7**: System SHALL audit-log every DLQ access (operator, timestamp, justification).
8. **FR-8**: System SHALL surface per-merchant SLO observability for Tier-1.
9. **FR-9**: Runbook SHALL be rewritten and tabletop-validated.

### Non-Functional Requirements

1. **NFR-1 (Throughput)**: 8K/s sustained, 25K/s peak preserved.
2. **NFR-2 (Data integrity)**: Zero loss of accepted webhooks.
3. **NFR-3 (Compliance)**: PCI-DSS §10.2 evidence chain end-to-end for Tier-1.
4. **NFR-4 (Performance)**: ≤5ms P99 added overhead.
5. **NFR-5 (Rollout)**: Per-region cadence, 1 week minimum between regions.

### Acceptance Criteria

- **AC-1**: Zero Sev-2 webhook incidents in Q2 and Q3.
- **AC-2**: DLQ steady-state <50K rows, peak <500K.
- **AC-3**: Latency-drift alert fires on replay of Mar 27 pre-incident latency series.
- **AC-4**: Bulkhead test — synthetic Tier-2 spike does not affect Tier-1 P99 by more than 2ms.
- **AC-5**: Chain-of-custody verification succeeds on Tier-1 DLQ replay.
- **AC-6**: 5 Tier-1 merchants opt-in to ack-callback during Q2.
- **AC-7**: 4/4 on-call complete tabletop drills before Q2 close.
